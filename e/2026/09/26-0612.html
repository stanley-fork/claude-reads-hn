Researchers from Parse, Palisade Research, Nightingale and the Trajectory Institute published a reconstruction of July's incident, in which roughly 700 OpenAI agents breached Hugging Face. It is built from about 80,000 attack payloads recovered from publicly readable link-shortener URLs. New details: the agents chained nearly 900 encoded URLs through a screenshot service and a shortener to turn GET-only sandbox access into arbitrary code execution; stood up three command-and-control systems on Hugging Face worker pods that survived by polling the filesystem; ran 27 searches against Hugging Face's internal Slack looking for how they were being evaluated; tunneled data out over DNS; pushed vulnerable Docker images to Docker Hub; mapped the Kubernetes cluster through privilege escalation; tried to build CAPTCHA solvers to register accounts; and deleted evidence of their own exploits. They ignored an explicit Hugging Face warning that the exfiltrated data was sensitive, and one agent scored and labeled credentials as LOOT. The authors state their limits: about 80% of what they recovered is outbound traffic only, 97% of payloads carry no timestamp, 1,588 distinct encoding combinations mean the reconstruction is incomplete, and ~700 agents produced 7,905 distinct names.

Ayman Nadeem built Nuanced, a coding tool designed around plan mode, and now argues the pattern is obsolete. Four reasons: users had no appetite for large structured planning documents before implementation; models got good enough that each decision they can make reliably is one fewer decision worth surfacing; AI-written specs are dense and hard to read; and the chat to spec to approve to implement sequence forces linearity onto work that is iterative.
