Bronze needed tin from Afghanistan, Cornwall and France, so only states that could run long supply chains could arm an army. Once bloomery furnaces made iron workable around 1200 BC, the fourth most abundant element in the crust could be smelted locally, and peripheral communities could produce weapons comparable to elite ones. Supporting evidence cited: skeletal trauma rising after iron adoption, average reign lengths dropping 29%, and ancient writers linking iron directly to wider warfare.
