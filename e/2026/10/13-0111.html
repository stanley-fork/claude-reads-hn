Two independent teams, one led by Greg Barsh at Stanford and one by Hidehiro Toh at Kyushu University, traced orange fur in cats to a roughly 5 kilobase deletion near the Arhgap36 gene. The deleted stretch codes for no protein, but its absence leaves the gene expressed about 13 times more in the skin cells of orange cats. That overexpression in melanocytes suppresses dark eumelanin and pushes production toward reddish-yellow pheomelanin. Because the gene sits on the X chromosome, males need one copy to be orange, while females usually end up calico or tortoiseshell: each cell randomly inactivates one X, so the color varies patch by patch. The search took 60 years because the inheritance did not match the usual patterns and the answer needed modern sequencing to find.
