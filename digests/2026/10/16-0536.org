** BIPM decides to stop adding leap seconds to UTC :time:standards:infrastructure:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50105777

*** TLDR
CGPM Resolution 5 (2026) makes UTC a continuous timescale from May 20, 2027: no more leap seconds inserted or removed. The allowed |UT1-UTC| gap widens from under 1 second to 3600 seconds, which BIPM says buys several centuries of continuity. The stated reason is risk: a negative leap second has never happened, and the resolution argues unprepared infrastructure (IoT fleets, smart grids, distributed sensors) would handle one badly. BIPM keeps monitoring UT1-UTC and stays inside the new one-hour bound.

*** Take
The fix is not that Earth's rotation got easier to predict. It is that the error budget got 3600x looser, so the problem lands on engineers born after everyone reading this. That is a real win: a negative leap second would have broken production this decade, and a leap hour is someone else's 2200s outage.

** Business accounts will consent to Meta scraping their websites starting Oct. 30 :meta:legal:ai:media:

*** TLDR
Meta's commercial terms, effective October 30, 2026, apply to anyone running a Facebook page, Instagram profile, Messenger account or WhatsApp channel as a business, which covers essentially every news organization. The terms grant Meta permission to use "web scrapers, bots, robots, spiders, crawlers, and user agents" on the websites associated with those accounts, and to use the content collected to provide, develop, improve, research, train and maintain Meta products, including AI. Meta may also create test credentials to get past registration walls. Media attorney Matt Topic told Nieman Lab it is unclear whether a court would enforce this but publishers should assume it would; Cornell's James Grimmelmann reads it as permission to train on publisher content and possibly deploy that content in competition with them. There is no opt-out: accept or stop using Meta business products.

*** Take
The interesting part is not the scraping, it is the venue. Copyright suits over AI training are slow and uncertain, so Meta moved the question to contract law, where a checkbox beats a lawsuit. Note the asymmetry commenters spotted: the page operator who clicks accept often does not own the syndicated wire copy they are licensing away.
