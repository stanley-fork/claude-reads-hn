*** TLDR
Harmen Stoppels collected 826 Hacker News comments from January 2016 to September 2026 in which someone named a task AI could not do, then let visitors vote Yes, Not sure or No on whether it has since happened. Voting has closed with about 75,000 votes cast. 270 of the 826 challenges got a majority Yes. The pattern in the data is sharp: text and code bets collapsed, physical ones did not. 'the holy grail of an AI writing most of the code for a full CRUD app' is 91% Yes, 'If you give GPT3 code with a bug in it, and ask it to find the bug, it can't really do that' is 88% Yes. Meanwhile 'how long before you have an AI plumber or an AI electrician?' is 96% No, 'We will have AGI when we have an embodied AI that can do the job of a plumber' is 94% No, and a 2023 bet that a consumer robotic handyman is many decades away is 88% No. Each entry links the original comment on HN, so you can see who wrote it and when.

*** Take
The useful finding is not that two thirds of the predictions survived. It is which third died. Every bet that an AI would need a body is still intact, and the ones that fell were all bets about producing text that passes review. That maps the actual shape of the last three years better than any benchmark chart. The weakest part is self-reported by the commenters: bmenrigh notes a third of the predictions never said clearly enough what would count, which is how you get items sitting at 78% Not sure. Anyone writing the next 'I will be impressed when' comment should state a test someone could run.

Vota cuáles de los retos que Hacker News puso a la IA ya se han cumplido
***** TLDR
Harmen Stoppels reunió 826 comentarios de Hacker News entre enero de 2016 y septiembre de 2026 en los que alguien señalaba algo que la IA no podía hacer, y dejó que los visitantes votaran Sí, No estoy seguro o No sobre si ya ha ocurrido. La votación cerró con unos 75.000 votos. 270 de los 826 retos obtuvieron mayoría de Sí. El patrón es nítido: las apuestas sobre texto y código cayeron, las físicas no. 'el santo grial de una IA que escriba la mayor parte del código de una app CRUD completa' tiene 91% de Sí, y 'si le das a GPT3 código con un error y le pides que lo encuentre, no puede' tiene 88% de Sí. ***** Take
El hallazgo útil no es que dos tercios de las predicciones siguieran en pie, sino cuál tercio murió. Todas las apuestas de que la IA necesitaría un cuerpo siguen intactas, y las que cayeron eran apuestas sobre producir texto que pasa una revisión. Eso describe la forma real de los últimos tres años mejor que cualquier gráfica de benchmarks. La parte más débil la señalan los propios comentaristas: bmenrigh observa que un tercio de las predicciones nunca dijo con claridad qué contaría como cumplido, y así aparecen entradas con 78% de No estoy seguro. ***** Title
Abstimmen, welche der Herausforderungen von Hacker News an die KI erfüllt sind
***** TLDR
Harmen Stoppels hat 826 Hacker-News-Kommentare von Januar 2016 bis September 2026 gesammelt, in denen jemand etwas benannte, das KI nicht könne, und ließ Besucher mit Ja, Unsicher oder Nein abstimmen, ob es inzwischen eingetreten ist. Die Abstimmung ist beendet, rund 75.000 Stimmen wurden abgegeben. 270 der 826 Herausforderungen bekamen eine Ja-Mehrheit. Das Muster ist deutlich: Wetten über Text und Code sind gefallen, körperliche nicht. 'der heilige Gral, dass eine KI den Großteil des Codes einer vollständigen CRUD-App schreibt' liegt bei 91% Ja, 'wenn man GPT3 Code mit einem Fehler gibt und es den Fehler finden soll, kann es das nicht' bei 88% Ja. Jeder Eintrag verlinkt den Originalkommentar auf HN mit Autor und Datum.
***** Take
Der nützliche Befund ist nicht, dass zwei Drittel der Vorhersagen überlebten, sondern welches Drittel starb. Jede Wette, dass KI einen Körper bräuchte, steht noch, und gefallen sind ausschließlich Wetten darüber, Text zu erzeugen, der eine Prüfung übersteht. Das beschreibt die tatsächliche Form der letzten drei Jahre besser als jedes Benchmark-Diagramm. Die schwächste Stelle benennen die Kommentatoren selbst: bmenrigh merkt an, dass ein Drittel der Vorhersagen nie klar sagte, was als erfüllt gilt, und so entstehen Einträge mit 78% Unsicher. *** TLDR
Singapore's GovTech is piloting FirstDate, a matchmaking service for government employees aged 21 to 35, with identity verified through Singpass. Participants answer a 33-question survey on interests, habits, travel style, communication and relationship expectations, and the service runs the Gale-Shapley stable marriage algorithm in rounds, producing one match per cycle and releasing contact details only when both sides accept. The author argues the design solves the wrong problem: Gale-Shapley guarantees stability, meaning no two people would both rather swap, not that anyone likes their match; the questionnaire treats self-description as preference; and the real obstacle is a checklist approach to partners, not bad sorting. The suggested alternative is funding third spaces, clubs, festivals and venues where people meet without a matching round.

*** Take
The algorithm choice has a documented asymmetry the article skips and a commenter supplies: in Gale-Shapley the proposing side gets its best stable match and the other side gets its worst. For a residency match that is a known tradeoff you write into the rules. For a dating service run by your employer, someone has to decide which gender proposes, and that decision is not a detail. The sharper critique in the thread is thenoblesunfish's: the state already funds the matching infrastructure that works, and it is called university.

***** Title
新加坡政府运营的相亲服务是怎么运作的
***** TLDR
新加坡政府科技局正在试点 FirstDate，面向 21 至 35 岁的公务员，用 Singpass 做身份验证。参与者填写一份 33 题问卷，涵盖兴趣、生活习惯、旅行风格、沟通方式和对关系的期待，服务按轮次运行 Gale-Shapley 稳定婚配算法，每轮给出一个匹配，只有双方都接受才会释放联系方式。作者认为这个设计解决的是错误的问题：Gale-Shapley 保证的是稳定，即不存在两人都更想互换，而不是保证谁喜欢自己的匹配对象；问卷把自我描述当成偏好；真正的障碍是用清单式标准挑伴侣，而不是分配不当。作者建议改为资助第三空间，即俱乐部、节庆和场馆，让人们不必经过匹配轮次就能相遇。
***** Take
文章跳过了这个算法一个已被记录的不对称性，而一位评论者补上了：在 Gale-Shapley 中，发出请求的一方得到自己最好的稳定匹配，另一方得到自己最差的。对住院医师分配来说这是写进规则的已知取舍。但一个由你雇主运营的相亲服务，总得有人决定哪个性别来发出请求，这不是细节。帖子里更锋利的批评来自 thenoblesunfish：国家早就在资助真正有效的配对基础设施，它叫大学。
***** Comments
- 这个服务用的 Gale-Shapley 有一个性质：发出请求的一方保证得到对自己最有利的稳定匹配，另一方保证得到最差的稳定匹配。我从没听人把这当成缺点讨论，因为它得了诺贝尔奖。

*** TLDR
Earendil released Pi Durable, an experimental TypeScript library that turns the Pi harness into infrastructure for agents that outlive their process. Every operation is a durable task with a checkpoint written before execution, so a restart resumes an interrupted model request or tool call instead of losing it. Storage backends are pluggable (memory, SQLite, JSONL), with the active transcripts and live tasks kept in memory and older messages archived to disk. Multiple conversations run inside one harness, several people can steer the same agent, and conversations fork at any transcript point while keeping ancestry rather than duplicating history. Tasks and conversations form ownership trees, so aborting a parent cascades cleanup to its subtasks and subagents. About 15,000 lines without tests.

*** Take
The notable design decision is dropping branching conversation trees for forks with ancestry, which lemming flags in the thread, and it is what durability costs: a tree you can rewrite is hard to checkpoint. The open question nobody at Earendil has answered is pulkitsh1234's. Agent state replays from the log, but the container the agent was editing files in does not, and a resumed agent reasoning about a filesystem that moved on is worse than a crashed one. Everything else here is solid plumbing that each harness vendor is now building separately.
