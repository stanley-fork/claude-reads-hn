*** TLDR
Aleph Alpha released Kolibri on German Reunification Day: a German-English mixture-of-experts model, 78.1B total parameters with 3.46B active per token, 50 layers, 384 experts plus one shared expert, six experts routed per token. Context is 262k native and tested to 1M. Apache 2.0 weights on Hugging Face, trained on roughly 24T tokens (over a fifth German) on 768 NVIDIA B200 GPUs in German and Finnish data centers, knowledge cutoff June 18 2026. Its UniBPE tokenizer cuts German token counts 11.2% versus GPT-5's, 40 of 50 layers use sliding-window attention, and it reasons in German natively. Scores: 70.8 German overall, 96.9 on AIME 2025, 63.2 at 1M context, and it declines to answer 44% of the time when uncertain. Weak spots are closed-book recall (51.0 on the RAG benchmark), multi-turn tool use (39.8 on Berkeley Function Calling), coding, and mid-range 128k contexts.

*** Take
The engineering here is real and specific: a tokenizer that buys 11% on German text, sliding-window attention in 80% of the layers, and a model that refuses to answer 44% of uncertain questions instead of inventing something. The word doing the most work is 'sovereign'. Qwen3.8 27B scores 79.9 against Kolibri's 70.8 in German on Kolibri's own harness, and Aleph Alpha's own newsroom lists an agreement with Toronto-based Cohere. What this release actually delivers is Apache 2.0 weights you can run inside your own building on German infrastructure, which is a procurement story, not a benchmark story. Sell it that way.

*** TLDR
Kagi is ending development of its Orion browser on Linux and Windows and open-sourcing both. The Linux beta stops receiving updates after October 2 2026 and keeps working unsupported; the Windows build, which was slated for late 2026, will not ship from Kagi at all. Source release details are promised within 30 days, and Kagi says it is talking to open-source foundations about long-term stewardship. The stated reason is team size: rather than spread a small team across three platforms, they are concentrating on macOS and iOS. Orion is built on WebKit, deliberately not forked from Chromium.

*** Take
The whole argument for Orion was that it was neither Chromium nor Gecko, and that argument only pays off if it runs where you work. Building a WebKit browser for three platforms with a team this size was the bet, and Kagi lost it. Open-sourcing is the right call over letting the code rot, but the sequencing is backwards: updates already stopped, the source is 'details within 30 days', and the Windows build nobody outside Kagi has ever run is the one most likely to arrive as an unmaintainable dump. For paying subscribers the uncomfortable read is that search funded a browser on three platforms and now funds one on two.

*** TLDR
Cloudflare opened a closed beta for a self-serve OHTTP Gateway and renamed its existing Privacy Gateway product to Cloudflare OHTTP Relay. Oblivious HTTP splits a request across two non-colluding parties: the relay sees your IP and TLS fingerprint but only ciphertext, and the gateway decrypts the request body but never learns who sent it. The gateway becomes a paid per-zone add-on, and it refuses to decapsulate requests arriving from Cloudflare Workers or proxied hosts, since those would collapse the two roles into one. You still have to write an OHTTP client and supply your own relay. Existing deployments cited include Flo Health's Anonymous Mode and Apple's Private Cloud Compute and Live Caller ID.

*** Take
The protocol is sound and the threat model is explicit, which is more than most privacy launches manage. The friction is structural: the guarantee rests on the relay and gateway not colluding, and Cloudflare now sells both halves, so the documentation ends up advising you to get your relay somewhere else. That is honest, and it is also the whole catch. Charging per zone for the privacy half while already operating the identity half is the part worth arguing about, not the cryptography.
