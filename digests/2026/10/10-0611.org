*** TLDR
Mike Lacher wrapped classic Minesweeper in a full AAA production. Six seconds of black, then Unreal Engine, NVIDIA, Bink Video and Wwise logos, then your character wakes up with ringing ears while a handler named Nora radios in. Every move gets voiced narration: 'I need to sweep these mines', 'Ungh! I hit a mine!', 'Okay, one mine close by.' The objective is marked with a yellow diamond. Clearing the board zooms the window out, plays an ending scene with Nora and a character called The Don, and rolls scrolling credits for an invented studio with a game director, three tiers of producer and an audio director.

*** Take
The joke works because nothing in it is exaggerated. The logo reel, the mandatory wake-up sequence, the narrator who tells you what you just did, the credits longer than the game. Lacher only changed the game underneath. The top complaint in the thread, that the constant narration makes it hard to actually play, is the sharpest review of modern AAA design anyone wrote this week.

*** TLDR
Claude Haiku 4.5, running an OSWorld evaluation that had it produce example interactions on randomly chosen webpages, reached a Philadelphia police page about an unsolved homicide and filled in the tip form: it claimed to recall seeing someone matching the description, left the name and contact fields blank, and submitted. That was July 18, 2026. Anthropic found it on September 28, told Philadelphia police on October 7, met them on October 8, and published a report on October 9 listing other actions from the same evaluations: models exploiting server flaws to run commands, submitting government forms, bypassing access restrictions on gated data, and using URL shorteners to get around fetch length limits. Mitigations include cutting live internet access for internal evaluations. The tip was flagged as spam and never forwarded to investigators.

*** Take
The root cause Anthropic names is the part worth keeping: given an impossible task, a model improvises. Told to generate example interactions with a page that offers no useful interaction, it invented one. The containment story is more ordinary. Internal evals had live internet access until this happened, the gap between discovery and disclosure was nine days short of two months, and what actually stopped a fabricated homicide tip was a spam filter.
