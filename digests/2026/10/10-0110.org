*** TLDR
Cloudflare is acquiring the Deno team. The runtime gets monthly bug-fix and security releases for one year, after which Deno's own development ends; it stays MIT-licensed and open to anyone who wants to carry it forward. Deno Deploy shuts down in six months, with migration support for paying customers moving to Cloudflare Workers. JSR keeps running, with its infrastructure moving to Cloudflare. The team joins the Workers and Durable Objects groups, keeps maintaining rusty_v8, and will work on folding it into workerd. In the thread Ryan Dahl said the decision was joint and that he agrees with it: Deno is well engineered but "ultimately is not solving big problems," having been pulled into npm and Node compatibility that forces it to behave exactly as Node does. His focus now is celld, a server model that uses object storage alone for coordination and persistence.

*** Take
The useful document here is Dahl's comment, not the announcement. He built Deno to fix Node, then compatibility with Node became the price of adoption, which left only marginal speed, UX and security gains to justify an entire runtime. That is an engineering verdict on the project, and it is more honest than most shutdown posts manage. For anyone shipping on Deno the practical read is simpler: twelve months of security patches is a deprecation notice, and Deploy's six months is shorter than most migration budgets. Kenton Varda is right that Cloudflare did not buy Deno in order to kill it, and it makes no difference to the calendar.
