*** TLDR
Permit Pending is a free browser city builder by Peter Cai where you are an elected mayor rather than a god. Every rezoning, bus lane and factory goes through public hearings, neighbour objections, lawsuits and appeals, and you need a seven-seat council plus re-election every four years. The economy underneath models resident incomes, business growth and land values. No win condition, saves stay local, and it works offline after the first load.

*** Take
The game's actual argument is that what blocks housing is procedure, not money or engineering, and a hearing queue you have to sit through makes that point better than another zoning-reform essay. The complaint about every button being available in minute one is fair: the tutorial should ration the UI the way the council rations your permits.

*** TLDR
Ofcom chief executive Melanie Dawes told a parliamentary committee on 20 October that if foreign courts block enforcement, the regulator will have to accept it cannot recover the money it fined 4chan under the Online Safety Act. In 4chan v. Ofcom, filed in the federal District Court for the District of Columbia, the court rejected 4chan's sovereign-immunity motion but noted in passing that American courts do not enforce foreign judgments that are penal in nature or that conflict with the First Amendment. Preston Byrne reads this as extraterritorial speech regulation hitting the US border, and pushes two bills, the GRANITE Act and the HOMEFRONT Act, that would strip sovereign immunity from foreign censors.

*** Take
Ofcom wrote a rule with global reach and no collection mechanism, and the answer to "how does a UK fine get paid by a company with no UK staff, no UK bank and no plans to visit" was always going to be: it doesn't. The expensive part is that every site which did comply spent real money on age checks while the one that ignored the letter is still up.
