*** TLDR
Lexifina reconstructed the edit history of the US Code by applying public laws in order to earlier text, so you can see which law changed a provision and read the text in force on any given date. They tested 365 changes across nine titles from 2017 onward: 361 reproduced exactly, four could not be fully confirmed. Of those 365, 261 took effect on enactment, 72 depended on separate effective-date notes, and 29 on dates buried inside the amendment itself. The post also cites a Munich study where models answered 24% of post-cutoff statutory questions correctly alone and 87% when handed the relevant text.

*** Take
The interesting number is not 361, it is the four they could not reproduce and the three different places an effective date can hide. Their own cited research says models reconstructing historical statute text land 93-99% visually right while matching exactly only half the time on single amendments and a fifth on chains, which in law is the difference between correct and wrong. Commenters want the Office of the Law Revision Counsel required to publish the repo itself, and that is the right ask: this should not have to be reverse engineered by a startup.

*** TLDR
Anthropic published results from work with six organizations. At Generate Biomedicines, six scientists used Claude Code to redesign part of a CAR T-cell receptor; one design was about 70% shorter and produced roughly ten times as many receptors. Divergence Labs had Claude generate about 35,000 antibody designs in a month targeting IgE detachment: three bound, twenty dislodged it, three beat the standard medication. BigHat tested 1,010 Claude-proposed variants in 10 weeks and reports more than 10x tighter binding, 18C higher heat stability and more than 100x slower clumping. Enveda used Claude on 1,061 pages of manufacturing and regulatory documentation, where it caught all 11 issues human reviewers found plus seven more and cut review from over 25 hours to under three. Anthropic also acquired Mithril Medicine and started its own drug programs. Everything described is preclinical.

*** Take
The most defensible numbers here are the paperwork ones. An 89% cut in regulatory document review is measurable and repeatable; "three designs outperformed the standard medication" is a binding assay, and binding assays have a famously poor conversion rate into approved drugs. Read the post's own ask as part of the result: Anthropic says plainly it needs to integrate Claude more deeply into more biotech firms, and it has bought a drug company and started in-house programs. This is a sales document that happens to contain data.

*** Comments
