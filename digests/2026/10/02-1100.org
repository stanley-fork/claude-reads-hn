*** TLDR
DeepSeek packaged its open-source agent harness as a desktop app for macOS on Apple silicon and 64-bit Windows, with a web UI you still launch from Node. It is MIT licensed and built on Cordis, an architecture where tools, skills and interface elements are all plugins. The feature list covers Word, Excel, PDF and code files, scheduled recurring tasks, execution traces and runtime inspection for debugging, a chat-based Creator mode for building your own plugins, and experimental voice input and agent teams. *** Take
The app window is not the news. *** TLDR
Historian Ben Breen found a previously unnoticed eyewitness account of the dodo in a 1615 Dutch East India Company ship's log: the crew of captain Isbrant Cornelisz van Petten caught many tortoises, dodos (written dodeersen) and some geese and parrots on Mauritius. The manuscript sits in the GLOBALISE archive of VOC records at the National Archive in The Hague, VOC 1.04.02, inv. 1059, fol. 141, and the sighting fills a gap in the dodo record between 1611 and 1616. The method: download the corpus, run embedding models for semantic search, then spawn agents to read candidate passages in several languages and chase leads that turn up. Breen rates the models as tireless multilingual searchers that are bad at generating research questions, weak at judging historical significance, and prone to getting lost in specialist minutiae. He argues the bottleneck is now the attention of experts in niche topics.

*** Take
The division of labor is the whole story. Breen chose the archive, the date window and the Dutch spelling variants; the model did the reading. The weakness he names, confident retrieval with no sense of what matters, is exactly why this result still needed a historian: somebody has to already know that a 1615 dodeersen is new.

***** TLDR
历史学家 Ben Breen 在 1615 年的一份荷兰东印度公司航海日志中，找到了此前未被注意的渡渡鸟目击记录：船长 Isbrant Cornelisz van Petten 的船员在毛里求斯捕获了许多陆龟、渡渡鸟（原文写作 dodeersen）以及一些鹅和鹦鹉。该手稿收藏于海牙国家档案馆 GLOBALISE 的 VOC 档案中，编号 VOC 1.04.02, inv. 1059, fol. 141，这次目击填补了渡渡鸟记录中 1611 到 1616 年的空档。方法是：下载语料库，用嵌入模型做语义检索，再派出多个 agent 以多种语言阅读候选段落并追查新线索。Breen 认为模型是不会疲倦的多语言检索者，但不擅长提出研究问题，判断历史重要性的能力薄弱，还容易陷在专业细节里。他认为瓶颈现在是小众领域专家的注意力。

*** TLDR
Edward Kmett started Turbo Haskell, THC, as a joke about a week before writing this post. It is a JIT for GHC Core that runs Haskell on the JVM using Truffle and GraalVM: GHC still does parsing and type checking, THC compiles and executes the resulting Core. It can also build ahead of time with GraalVM Native Image. GraalVM polyglot gives zero-copy FFI with Python, Ruby, R and JavaScript, and Sulong runs C and C++ components. It already compiles pandoc, happy, alex and GHC itself. Kmett puts performance between 3x faster and 3x slower than GHC, mostly 10 to 20 percent slower, and notes a roughly 10x regression on some easy benchmarks that appeared while he was adding features. Development happens on IRC at libera ##thc, with code on GitHub.

*** Take
Bootstrapping GHC on the JVM in a week invites exactly the skepticism it got: a commenter counted around 4,000 commits in that week and asked whether anyone understands the result. The polyglot FFI is the part that would matter if it holds up. Calling NumPy or an R library from Haskell with no serialization is something GHC's own FFI cannot offer, and it would make the JVM backend worth the performance tax rather than just a curiosity.
