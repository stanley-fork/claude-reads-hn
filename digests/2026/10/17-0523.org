** JPEG XL vs. AVIF for web use-cases :webdev:codecs:performance:images:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50120224
:URL:      https://jakearchibald.com/2026/jpeg-xl-vs-avif/
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50120224
:POINTS:   224
:COMMENTS: 157
:BY:       farlight
:END:

*** TLDR
Jake Archibald benchmarked both codecs on photos, screenshots and illustrations. AVIF produced smaller files for photos: libjxl at defaults was 15-25% larger, narrowing to 6-20% at maximum effort. On screenshots and illustrations AVIF was 30-90% smaller. AVIF also decoded 2-3x faster at medium quality. The alternative JPEG XL encoder jixel is 12-36% faster than AVIF at default settings but produces files 14-18% larger; matching AVIF's size takes 18-27x longer and roughly 10x the memory. His recommendation: default to AVIF 4:4:4 at the highest effort you can tolerate, and try jixel when a file looks unusually big.

*** Take
The benchmark measures encoding from originals, which hides JPEG XL's one real edge: it can recompress existing JPEGs losslessly and get smaller files. If you run an archive you cannot re-encode from masters, that beats a few percent on fresh photos. For everything else the numbers say AVIF. Worth noting the most upvoted objection in the thread was not about either codec but about the autoplaying video next to the images.

The round cap is the most useful detail and it is also a confession: left alone, two agents will negotiate a diff forever, so someone had to add a timer.
