:PROPERTIES:

*** TLDR
Denmark's Central Person Register says someone abused a Danish company's lawful CPR lookup access to pull names, addresses and CPR numbers on about 8.8 million registered people. That is effectively everyone alive in the register plus foreign nationals who have been resident. People who registered name and address protection were excluded. CPR revoked the company's access, reported it to the data protection authority Datatilsynet and to police, and has an investigation running with outside specialists.

*** Take
Nothing was hacked. A vendor with a legal query interface into the national identity database was the entire attack surface, and 8.8 million lookups went through it without tripping anything. The deeper problem is that the CPR number works as an authenticator at Danish banks and insurers, so a national ID that is both public and a password holds up exactly until the first leak.

*** TLDR
Huawei and Qualcomm signed a multi-year cross-license covering patents in 5G, compute, AI and networking, and Qualcomm is separately buying a set of Huawei's US patents in those same areas. No financial terms were disclosed and the purchase still needs regulatory approval. Both sides restated their FRAND commitments: Huawei's Alan Fan pointed to decades of its own R&D in mobile communications, Qualcomm's John Han framed the deal as recognition of Qualcomm's 5G standard-essential patent licensing program.

*** Take
Both press quotes declare victory, which is how you know the net payment direction changed and neither party wants to name it. Huawei stopped buying Qualcomm modems after the Kirin 9000, so the chip-revenue half of that relationship is gone and only the patent half is left. The more consequential line is Qualcomm buying Huawei's US patents outright: it moves them to a licensor that can actually enforce them in American courts.

*** TLDR
CedarDB implemented Doom's game logic and its renderer in SQL: about 5,900 lines for the game and 1,300 lines across 89 CTEs for the 320x200 renderer. Recursive CTEs handle BSP traversal and line of sight, window functions reproduce Doom's imperative ceilingclip and floorclip by sorted aggregation, and a single query returns the whole framebuffer as a 192,000-byte bytea. A Python client only polls the keyboard, drives the 35 Hz tick and blits to pygame. They measure 2.15 ms per game tic and roughly 13 ms per frame on a Ryzen 7 7840U, and admit BSP rendering is a bad fit for SQL: their earlier raycasting version was simpler to express and faster per pixel.

*** Take
The postmortem is the valuable part. Two wins are real: every weapon and monster is a table row you can change without recompiling, and multiplayer consistency falls out of transactions with database roles as the anti-cheat. The renderer is the confession. Because set semantics cannot mutate a z-buffer in place, they generate every candidate pixel and resolve the winner with MIN() over a packed bigint. It works, it hits frame budget, and it is three times the work the C version does.
