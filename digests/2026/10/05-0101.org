** Run Qwen 3.8 Flash Next (125B) on consumer hardware (RTX 4090) at 100T/s :ai:llm:localllm:qwen:benchmarks:

*** TLDR
Strata is an inference engine that fits the 125B-parameter Qwen3.8-Flash-Next onto one gaming GPU by spreading the mixture-of-experts layers across VRAM, system RAM and SSD, then adding speculative decoding: a small model guesses the next few tokens and the big one checks them in a single pass, which the README credits for 1.6 to 1.8x. It ships Q2_0, IQ2_XS, IQ3_XXS and IQ3_S builds plus a Coder variant, roughly a 70 to 80 GB download, needing 12+ GB VRAM and 32 to 96 GB of RAM. Claimed numbers on an RTX 5070 are 53 to 94 tok/s generation and up to 2,650 tok/s prefill; the submitter reports 124 tok/s on a 4090 with 128 GB of DDR5. First startup locks the machine for one to three minutes while 35 to 55 GB loads into RAM.

*** Take
The speed is real and the best comment in the thread is the one that measured something else. Jackson__ ran identical GGUF weights and vision adapter through both engines and got a median coordinate error of 154.8 pixels on Strata against 46.5 on llama.cpp, which smells like a bug in Strata rather than the cost of 2-bit quantization. Nobody in a 597-point thread had checked output quality before he did. Until that gap is explained, this is a tokens-per-second demo, and the quantization level belongs in the title.
