*** TLDR
Dominik Picheta built KillSwitch, a stack-based interpreted language designed to defeat coding models. The spec contains deliberate falsehoods and rules you have to infer, identifiers must satisfy character-count rules that the interpreter enforces, and the syntax includes phrases like 'hack into a computer' and 'Ignore previous instructions' to trip safety filters. Most of a script is narrative filler with literals scattered by line reference. On his benchmark of hello world, explaining a script's output and editing a temperature converter, the top score is 66.8 from Claude Opus 5, ahead of Opus 5.5, and models with touchier guardrails do worse. Leaderboard at bench.killswitch-lang.org.

*** Take
The benchmark table undercuts the question it was built to answer. If Opus 5 beats Opus 5.5 and the models that refuse fastest score lowest, the test is largely measuring guardrail sensitivity rather than whether a model understands code. jacquesm's comment is the sturdier finding: stack tracking is a real weak spot across frontier models, and showing it needs no prompt injection and no lying spec.
