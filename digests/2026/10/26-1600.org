*** TLDR
Anthropic filed to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. Revenue: $41M in 2023, $386M in 2024, $4.585B in 2025, and $16.286B in the first half of 2026 alone. Operating loss for that half was $693M. The $80.7B headline net loss is almost entirely a non-cash remeasurement of convertible notes, SAFEs and derivatives, worth $72.4B on its own. The company has raised $141.1B and holds $112.4B in cash plus $19.3B in short-term investments. Aggregate committed compute spend is redacted in this draft, running to 2045, but commenters pulled per-provider figures for Google, AWS, Broadcom and SpaceX that add up to roughly $500B. A single Class F share held by a founder LLC carries 50.1% of the voting power, and the Long-Term Benefit Trust elects a majority of the board, so Anthropic lists as a controlled company. Share count and price range are still blank.

*** Take
Two numbers matter and neither is the headline. The $80.7B net loss is an artifact of marking its own funding rounds to market. The real result is a $693M operating loss on $16.3B of revenue, which is a startling place to be at this scale. What the filing does not resolve is the distance between that and roughly half a trillion in compute commitments running to 2045, in a market where the commoditization argument gets more evidence every month. Class A buyers get to watch that play out, not vote on it.

*** TLDR
Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, roughly a quarter of its workforce, which the Seattle Times frames as an attempt to control headcount while avoiding layoffs. The article is mostly paywalled past the lede; the terms surfaced in the HN thread. The offer goes to Level 70 and below in the US, UK, Canada and India, excludes sales, marketing and data center roles, and pays up to 39 weeks of base pay. Microsoft is calling it a Voluntary Exit Offer and bundling career planning resources with it. Eligible staff get individual offer details by email.

*** Take
Offering 60,000 buyouts is not an alternative to layoffs, it is a layoff with an opt-in phase in front of it. The number offered sits far above the number wanted because nobody knows the acceptance rate in advance, and whatever gap is left after the deadline gets closed involuntarily. Thirty-nine weeks is a genuinely good package, which is exactly the problem: the people who can get hired somewhere else tomorrow are the first to take it.
