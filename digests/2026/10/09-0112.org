*** TLDR
OpenAI posted a preprint claiming the Partition Principle does not imply the Axiom of Choice, an open question for over a century. Asaf Karagila, a set theorist who has worked on choice for 15 years and once offered a bottle of whisky for a solution, says he will not verify it. His objections are about the writing, not the claim: strange structure, off terminology, unexpected lemmas, and citations to unpublished lecture notes instead of peer-reviewed work, with no apparent input from anyone in the field. He calls it a denial-of-service attack on the mathematical community and argues the onus to communicate clearly sits with the author.

*** Take
Karagila is making a narrower point than the comment section heard. He is not refusing to read AI output; he is refusing to do unpaid editorial work on a paper whose authors could afford an expert reviewer and skipped one. A proof nobody can check is not a contribution yet, and the asymmetry is the problem: OpenAI generates in minutes what costs specialists weeks to referee.

It runs on the stock picture-elements card, with state_image swapping a still frame for an animated WebP when the AC turns on, a helper entity flipping day/night at sunrise and sunset, and Browser Mod for custom pop-ups. The post does not say what it cost.

*** Take
The interesting work here is asset specification, not illustration.
