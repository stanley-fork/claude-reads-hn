*** TLDR
Attackers reached Danfoss refrigeration controllers at supermarkets in France, the Netherlands and Belgium on October 12 and ran defrost cycles continuously at 50C instead of the usual two hours a day. Intermarche, Leclerc, Coopérative U, Albert Heijn, Etos, Gall & Gall and Delhaize were hit; electronic shelf labels, deliveries and loyalty programs also broke. Early French losses were put at 300,000 euros with national totals expected in the millions. Albert Heijn shut systems down preventively. No attribution. Claroty's Team82 had already published three bugs in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A line, including an authentication bypass via a hidden code-of-the-day (CVE-2025-41450), fixed in firmware R4.3.1, and counted roughly 2,765 of these controllers exposed on the public internet.

*** Take
The fix shipped before the attack. Danfoss sells internet-connected case controllers on the promise that a technician never has to drive out to start a defrost, and that same feature run in reverse is what spoiled the food. Supermarkets run on thin margins and nobody there budgets for a firmware upgrade on a box that has worked for fifteen years, so the exposure sits there until someone scans for it.

** Codex CLI installs a persistent, self-updating background daemon by default :openai:devtools:cli:privacy:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50060694
:URL:      https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/48195
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50060694
:POINTS:   448
:COMMENTS: 229
:BY:       whimblepop
:END:

*** TLDR
Since version 0.157.0 the Codex CLI starts `codex app-server --managed-daemon` for eligible interactive sessions. It copies about 374 MB of platform files into ~/.codex/packages/app-server-daemon/, spawns an updater that checks hourly, survives closing the terminal, and pins the environment of whichever session started it first, so proxy settings and working directory leak across sessions. The release notes covered it in one line about automatic background-server startup. There is no documented setting, only the feature flag daemon_auto_start, and `codex app-server daemon stop` leaves the updater loop running. The issue is open with no maintainer reply.

*** Take
A background process that reinstalls itself every hour, keeps running after you close the terminal, and ignores the documented stop command is the part people are reacting to, not the idea of a daemon. Running an app server for interactive sessions is reasonable engineering. Turning it on by default, behind an undocumented flag, with a stop command that does not stop it, is how you get your own users comparing your CLI to malware.
