*** TLDR
An Arkham Intelligence entity page labeled "Binance-hacker-1029" tracks about $5.6B drained from Binance on October 29, the largest crypto theft on record. Tether and Circle froze roughly $2.4B of it within hours: 971.15M USDT and 823M USDC on Ethereum, 312.73M USDT on Tron, plus smaller blocks. CZ confirmed it "does look like a hack on Binance" and said accountholders would be made whole. The SAFU insurance fund holds about $1B in BTC against the loss. No attack vector has been published. Bitcoin fell to roughly $79,000. Polymarket disclosed a separate security incident the same day. (Arkham's page is behind a bot check; figures here come from the thread and exchange statements.)

*** Take
The recovery is the embarrassing part. $2.4B came back because two companies can freeze balances by fiat, which is exactly the custodial power crypto was built to remove, and nobody in the thread is complaining. The SAFU number is the other tell: a $1B fund against hot wallets holding billions was never sized for this, it was sized for marketing. Binance can cover the gap from revenue, so depositors are probably fine and the architecture is still the same tomorrow.

*** TLDR

He joined the Royal Shakespeare Company in 1966 and spent two decades in classical theatre before playing Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation from 1987 to 1994, and later in films and the series Picard. He played Charles Xavier across seven X-Men films from 2000 to 2017. He was appointed OBE in 2001, knighted in 2010, and won two Olivier Awards and a Grammy. His memoir Making It So was published in 2023. Walter Koenig, who played Chekov in the original series, died days earlier.

Martin Fowler names "LLM voice" as the reader's sense that prose came from a model, an uncanny valley effect he admits he cannot pin to specific markers and is not sure he can reliably detect. He dismisses detection tools on two grounds: he doubts their accuracy, and the reaction he cares about is a human one, so a verdict from software misses the point. His claim is that the artifacts survive editing and are very hard to remove once a draft has them. His rules: an LLM may help with an outline, but "authors should fill the prose themselves"; his site requires disclosure when a writer used AI assistance; he uses LLMs himself only to check facts. The underlying worry is that delegating writing means delegating thinking.

*** Take
Fowler's argument is narrower than the thread that formed around it. He is not claiming LLM prose is detectable; he is saying detectability is irrelevant because the reaction lives in the reader.
