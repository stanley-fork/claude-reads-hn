** A 10-year-old CNN outperforms frontier LLMs for skin cancer diagnosis :ai:machine-learning:healthcare:research:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50233779
:URL:      https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50233779
:POINTS:   111

:BY:       sungam

*** TLDR
Dermatologist Magnus Lynch ran 200 biopsy-proven lesions from the MILK10k dataset past GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1 and Google's 2015 Inception v3, the last fine-tuned for about 20 minutes on 68,011 ISIC images on a single H100. The CNN got 125/200 diagnoses right (62%) against 42% for both LLMs, found 13 of 30 melanomas against 8 and 11, and correctly cleared 37 of 50 benign moles against 16 and 33. Test lesions and every other image of the same patient were held out of training.

*** Take
62% is not a product either, and Lynch does not claim it is. The number that matters is the price: twenty minutes on one GPU beat two frontier models, so anyone routing an image classification problem through a chat API is paying more for worse. The counter-example in the thread, SkinGPT-4 at 80%, is a fine-tuned vision model too, which is the same point.

*** Comments

*** TLDR
Richard Borcherds, the Fields medalist at UC Berkeley, has stopped grading math homework for correctness and now awards credit for submission alone, because students can get full marks from AI tools. He says paying readers to check accuracy no longer makes sense, calls completion-based credit 'not a very good system' and says he has no better one. He also says he now hesitates to encourage students into mathematics careers.

*** Take
The sharpest comment in the thread points out Borcherds was not grading the homework himself; paid readers were. What ended is a labour pipeline, not a professor's workflow. The obvious replacement, short oral exams on the submitted work, is also the one nobody is funding, because it costs more staff hours per student than the system AI just broke.
