*** TLDR
Anthropic shipped Haiku 5.5 at $0.10 per MTok input and $0.50 output for prompts under 100,000 tokens, jumping to $0.50 and $2.50 above that line. Haiku 4.5 was a flat $1 and $5. The capability jump is large where agents care: OSWorld 2.1 computer use goes from 15.7% to 72.4%, Terminal-Bench 4.0 from 0.0% to 39.2%, Humanity's Last Exam with tools from 18.7% to 57.4%, against Sonnet 5.5 at 83.9%, 70.6% and 64.5%. Sonnet 5.5 cache reads were also halved to $0.10 per MTok, and Max 5x, Max 20x and Team subscribers now get $100, $200 and up to $500 of monthly API credit.

*** Take
The 100k-token cliff is the story, not the headline price. Haiku's pitch is high-volume subagent work inside coding harnesses, and that is precisely the workload whose context blows past 100k by mid-task, so the cheap tier describes the demo and the expensive tier describes the job. No other model in the lineup has this cutoff, which suggests it exists to stop people from using the cheapest model for long-context work rather than to reflect what serving it costs.

*** TLDR
Margaret Hamilton died on September 30 at the age of 90; MIT announced it on October 7. At MIT's Instrumentation Lab she directed the more than 400 people who built the Apollo flight software. She designed the priority-driven scheduling that let the Apollo 11 guidance computer drop lower-priority work when it raised the 1202 overload alarm during the lunar descent, so the landing continued. She used the term software engineering to establish the work as engineering alongside hardware, wrote more than 130 publications, founded Higher Order Software in 1976 and later Hamilton Technologies, whose main product was the Universal Systems Language, and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016.

*** Take
The 1202 alarm remains the clearest case on record of a system built to shed load rather than fail: the computer was overloaded during the most unforgiving minutes of the mission and the descent went on. Hamilton also had to argue for the name of the discipline before she could argue for its methods, which is the part most retellings leave out.

***** Title
Ha muerto Margaret Hamilton
***** TLDR
Margaret Hamilton murió el 30 de septiembre a los 90 años; el MIT lo anunció el 7 de octubre. En el Instrumentation Lab del MIT dirigió a más de 400 personas que construyeron el software de vuelo del programa Apolo. Diseñó la planificación por prioridades que permitió al ordenador de guiado del Apolo 11 descartar tareas de menor prioridad cuando lanzó la alarma de sobrecarga 1202 durante el descenso lunar, de modo que el aterrizaje continuó. Usó el término ingeniería de software para situar el trabajo como ingeniería a la par del hardware, firmó más de 130 publicaciones, fundó Higher Order Software en 1976 y después Hamilton Technologies, cuyo producto principal fue el Universal Systems Language, y recibió la Medalla Presidencial de la Libertad en 2016.
***** Take
La alarma 1202 sigue siendo el caso documentado más claro de un sistema construido para soltar carga en lugar de fallar: el ordenador se sobrecargó en los minutos menos indulgentes de la misión y el descenso continuó.
