*** TLDR
A developer reports that the macOS 15.8.2 release candidate breaks OpenGL context creation: the drawable fails and glGetError returns 0x8cd5 on code that still works on 26.6. An Apple engineer replied, logged it as FB25133383, said he could not comment on known issues or on whether a fix might ship, and asked the reporter to retest each new build and add results to the ticket. 15.8.2 is a security-only point update to Sequoia, which shipped two years ago. ［Apple's forum sits behind a bot check, so this is from the HN thread, which quotes the exchange.］

*** Take
Two true things are getting mixed together. Breaking an API in a release candidate is what release candidates exist to catch, and a security-only patch to a two-year-old OS reaching RC with graphics broken means nobody ran that regression first. The genuinely bad part is smaller than the headline and worse than the defence: Apple asked an unpaid reporter to retest every build while declining to say whether it had touched the subsystem between them.

** Will it be a golden age or a dark age for mathematics (and science)? :mathematics:ai:academia:research:

:POINTS:   84

:BY:       smilelamp

*** TLDR
A guest post by Francesco Maggi on Terence Tao's blog, not by Tao himself. Maggi grants that LLMs produce correct Lean proofs and are real research tools, then argues the risk is not being out-proved but losing human understanding: long Lean proofs translate badly into readable mathematics, and the field's value has always come from mathematization, inventing new language, more than from clearing existing conjectures. His sharpest point is about training. The mechanism by which young mathematicians earn recognition has collapsed, because nobody can tell which part of a result was the person. He ends with a concrete ask: universities, societies and funding agencies should jointly build and govern frontier-grade models with transparency and confidentiality guarantees, rather than leaving private firms to decide which questions get the best tools.

*** Take
The interesting claim is the one that gets skipped in the comments. Mathematics reproduces itself through an apprenticeship that runs on attribution, and attribution is the thing AI breaks first, well before it breaks proof. Maggi's remedy, publicly governed frontier models, is also the only part of the post with no plan behind it: nobody has said who pays for a compute budget that competes with Google.

***** Title

***** TLDR
这是 Francesco Maggi 发在陶哲轩博客上的客座文章，不是陶本人所写。Maggi 承认大模型能写出正确的 Lean 证明、是真正的研究工具，随后指出风险不在于被机器证得更快，而在于人类理解的流失：长的 Lean 证明很难翻译成可读的数学，而数学的价值一直更多来自“数学化”，即发明新的语言，而不是攻克已有猜想。他最尖锐的一点关于人才培养：年轻数学家获得认可的机制已经崩塌，因为没人分得清一项成果中哪部分出自本人。他最后提出具体主张：大学、学会和资助机构应联合建设并治理具备前沿能力、兼顾透明度与保密性的模型，而不是让私营公司决定哪些问题配得上最好的工具。
***** Take
最值得注意的论点恰恰是评论区跳过的那个。数学靠一种以署名为基础的师徒制自我延续，而署名正是 AI 最先破坏的东西，远早于它破坏证明本身。Maggi 开出的药方——由公共机构治理的前沿模型——也是全文唯一没有配套方案的部分：没人说得出谁来出钱，支撑一份能和谷歌竞争的算力预算。

The current recommended build, PIKA V0.02, gets about 1 micron repeatable positioning inside an 8mm cube using 400-step NEMA17 steppers driving flexure stages derived from the OpenFlexure microscope project. It prints UV nail gel resin through etched wire or hypodermic needle probes, with droplets down to 4 microns and cured lines at 9 microns. *** Take
The claim worth attention is not the resolution, it is that the resolution comes from flexures and off-the-shelf stepper motors instead of anything exotic. The RepRap half stays aspirational, and the thread says why: a mechanism that works across 8mm does not shrink three orders of magnitude, because surface forces grow relative to mass and the square-cube law does not negotiate. Commenters also point out you can buy 1 micron glass scale encoders for under fifty dollars, which is true and misses that nobody was selling the whole machine.
