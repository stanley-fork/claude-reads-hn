*** TLDR
Paul Ford and Rich Ziade built nine free browser tools: word processor, outliner, presentation builder, spreadsheet, calculator, drawing tool, diagram maker, animator, calendar and trip planner. The entire document is stored in the URL hash fragment, so there is no login and no server-side copy. Sharing means sending a link. Export goes to PDF, Markdown, or a QR code containing the whole document, and longer files run through a URL shortener with browser-side encryption. It is MIT licensed, and the site publishes an llms.txt so agents can generate document URLs directly. It is the revival of TinySheet, which launched six years ago and then fell off the web.

*** Take
Keeping the document in the fragment is the product, not a trick. There is no account to delete, no retention policy to read, and nothing for a future owner to shut down, which is precisely what happened to TinySheet the first time. The limitation is the same thing: a URL has a length, and no amount of browser-side encryption on the shortener changes who holds the short link.

The post also opens a set of experimental flags it calls the danger zone and says are not reliable for production: images in state, instructions or criteria behind a TypeSafe-Danger-Zone-Images header, a new Box return type that gives a single bounding box, and longer context than production normally allows behind a second header. *** Take

*** TLDR
A catalogue of places where GCC, Clang and musl do not do what the C standard says. GCC hardcodes FLT_ROUNDS to 1 even though the standard says it tracks the runtime floating-point environment.
