** A tiny world model running games on a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W :ml:embedded:worldmodels:raspberrypi:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50295450

:POINTS:   219

*** TLDR
Two int8 networks on a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W: a looped transformer (one block applied one to four times, 26k to 131k parameters) that generates the next game frame, and an MLP actor (9k to 16k parameters) that plays inside those generated frames. No game code runs at inference. Five games ship: Breakout, Pong, Flappy Bird, Pac-Man lite and Doom-lite, at 2 to 8M multiply-accumulates per frame and 227 to 460 KB of the chip's 512 KB SRAM. Next-frame accuracy runs from 99.99% on Breakout down to 83.6% on Doom-lite. The world model is trained with quantization-aware training on 1 to 3M transitions, the actor with Stream AC(λ), and core 1 runs the simulation while core 0 drives USB and display.

*** Take
The number to stare at is 26k parameters for 99.99% frame accuracy on Breakout. World models get demoed at Genie scale, which makes it easy to assume the capability needs the scale, when most of what the big demos are paying for is the complexity of the environment rather than the mechanism. Doom-lite at 83.6% is where that stops being a free lunch, and the looped-transformer detail says the same thing: depth only became necessary when the game did.
