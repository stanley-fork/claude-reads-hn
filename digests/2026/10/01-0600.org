*** TLDR
Titan, an internal Microsoft analytics service, did not verify JWT signatures. The researcher, Faav, built an unsigned token with "alg":"none" and set the upn claim to "admin", which granted administrator SQL access across 17 connected analytics databases: about 17.3 trillion rows, 9,863 distinct table names, roughly 25,000 employee and account records with emails and login history, and Bing search activity with country and state level location. Faav ran two one-row queries to confirm access, extracted no customer PII, and received $5,000. Microsoft reviewed the writeup before publication and had sections and figures removed.

*** Take
The bug is alg:none, in 2026, at Microsoft, on a service wired to 17 databases. The $5,000 against a published program ceiling of $250,000, plus a pre-publication edit pass that cut figures, is how a company prices a report it would rather nobody read closely.

*** TLDR
Works in Progress argues the real puzzle is not why Bronze Age empires fell but why nothing reconsolidated for centuries. Bronze needed tin from Afghanistan, Cornwall and France, so only states that could run long supply chains could arm an army. Once bloomery furnaces made iron workable around 1200 BC, the fourth most abundant element in the crust could be smelted locally, and peripheral communities could produce weapons comparable to elite ones. Supporting evidence cited: skeletal trauma rising after iron adoption, average reign lengths dropping 29%, and ancient writers linking iron directly to wider warfare. Drought, famine and the Sea Peoples are treated as shocks that explain the fall but not the long silence.

*** Take
Splitting the collapse from the failure to recover is the useful move here, because the Sea Peoples story never explained the centuries of silence. The weak point is the one randomImmigrant names in the thread: the Harappans had tin nearby and bronze in ordinary houses with little visible hierarchy, which the supply-chain-equals-centralization argument has to account for.

***** TLDR
El bisabuelo del abuelo del autor era herrador en la Francia rural: herraba caballos y reparaba carros.
