*** TLDR
Cactus Compute released Whistle, a 16.9 MB open speech recognition model that runs on CPU with no dependencies: eight Simple Attention encoder blocks, eight laddered decoder blocks, 16 kHz mono input up to 30 seconds. It transcribes seven languages (English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Polish) with automatic language detection, reaches 11.1 ms to first token and 1,319 tokens/sec decoding on an M4 Pro CPU, and beats Whisper Base (145.3 MB) on LibriSpeech test-clean/other, SPGISpeech and Earnings-22 while losing to it on TED-LIUM and AMI. Builds ship for seventeen targets including RISC-V, MIPS, watchOS, the browser and WASI. It loads into the same C++ engine as the company's Needle model, so one binary can turn a clip into a transcript plus function calls.

*** Take
The size is the headline and the benchmark table is the content. Whistle wins on read speech and finance audio, loses on TED talks and AMI meeting recordings, which is the exact split you get from a small model trained on clean data. Meeting audio with crosstalk and accents is where transcription actually gets used, and that is where skolos measured it well behind a 1.7B Qwen ASR model over 170 messages. That comparison is unfair on purpose: 16.9 MB on a watch and 1.7B on an RTX 5080 are not the same product. The seventeen build targets are the real claim here, not the word error rate.

*** TLDR
A blog post arguing DeepSeek 4.1 Flash delivers frontier quality at a small fraction of frontier price. The author says that during normal work sessions they could not tell whether they were on DeepSeek or Opus, runs 4.1 Flash as their primary model with Claude Opus 5.5 reserved for final code review, and reports complex single queries costing about $0.003 and whole sessions staying under $1 on a $10/month OpenCode Go subscription. The mechanism credited is KV cache size, roughly 437x smaller than DeepSeek V1, which makes all-day sessions cheap to keep in context. The conclusion: Chinese labs will take the market because the same workloads cost far less.

*** Take
The post compares API prices and the thread answers with subscription prices, and that mismatch is the whole story. Nobody on a $100 or $200 monthly plan feels per-token cost, so a model that is orders of magnitude cheaper per token is competing against a number its customers never see. vishvananda burned $50 on OpenRouter in a few days and noted that is a quarter of a Codex subscription that would have absorbed more tokens than that. mlinsey ran the controlled version: GLM 5.3 on a $100/mo Z.ai plan against Opus 5.5 on a $100/mo Claude plan, no big gap. giancarlostoro's VRAM table covers the other exit, since 1,664 GB at FP16 means you are renting someone's cluster either way. The freak-out is scheduled for whenever the subsidies end, not for when the benchmarks land.
