** Any window from your Mac, on your iPhone and iPad :macos:ios:remote-desktop:open-source:

*** TLDR
sill is an Apache-2.0 macOS and iOS app that streams one Mac window, or the whole desktop, to an iPhone or iPad and sends touch, Apple Pencil, trackpad and keyboard input back. Capture goes through ScreenCaptureKit, encodes as HEVC, and travels over TLS 1.3 after a one-time pairing; the link can be local network, USB cable, peer-to-peer Wi-Fi, or a VPN such as Tailscale. No servers, no account. It needs Apple silicon and macOS 14+, iOS or iPadOS 17+, and Screen Recording, Accessibility and Local Network permissions. Limits: one window at a time, no audio, text-only clipboard capped at 256 KB, and the Mac has to stay awake.

*** Take
The streaming is not the interesting part, the scoping is. Sidecar and VNC both hand you an entire desktop; sill hands you one window with Pencil input, which is what a 6-inch screen can actually use. Apache 2.0 and no server dependency are why this one gets installed instead of bookmarked.

*** TLDR
Polygon's impressions after a month and more than 20 hours with Thekla's new puzzle game, the one marketed as 500 hours long. It remakes four indie Sokoban-style games, from Alan Hazelden, Silver Spaceship Software and Jonah Ostroff, as the four cardinal directions of a single open world, then combines their mechanics in the regions where those directions meet. The writer calls the puzzle design brilliant and the fog-covered overworld a puzzle in its own right, with standout chambers like a dragon whose fiery breath you redirect through mirrors. The complaint is structural: the difficulty only escalates, the reward for a hard puzzle is a harder puzzle, the stitched-in storytelling gives little reason to push on, and the review build still had placeholder voice acting. No verdict is offered. Masterpiece or exercise in exhaustion, ask again next month.

*** Take
A reviewer who still cannot decide after a month is the most useful thing written about this game so far. The 500 hours is not a content number, it is a design choice: most puzzle games dial difficulty back down after teaching you a rule, and this one does not. That is a legitimate preference for a specific audience, and it is the exact opposite of what made The Witness work for everyone else.
