*** TLDR
Virginia Tech's Fralin Biomedical Research Institute, publishing in Nature Metabolism, served adults two meals matched on weight, calories, macronutrients, fibre, sodium and water, differing only in degree of processing. 57 adults aged 18 to 45 enrolled and 32 completed both the metabolic chamber and the fMRI session. The ultraprocessed meal produced a markedly higher insulin response and higher blood glucose, and ventral striatum and nucleus accumbens responses to food images shifted in ways that tracked the metabolic difference, even though participants reported no preference between the two meals. One meal pair, young healthy subjects, small sample.

*** Take
The press release leaves out the detail that makes the result interesting, and a commenter supplies it: the meals were matched on glycaemic index and load as well, within 1.6 percent on every measured variable. That moves the insulin gap away from sugar content and towards the physical structure of the food. It is still 32 people and a single meal, so this is a mechanism worth chasing, not dietary advice.

** Retrofitting language models to operate over bytes :ml:llm:tokenization:research:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50001234
:URL:      https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11111-4
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50001234
:POINTS:   37
:COMMENTS: 3
:BY:       theanonymousone
:END:

*** TLDR
A Nature paper from Allen AI and collaborators describes byteification: converting an existing subword language model to run on raw bytes without retraining it from scratch. A local encoder and decoder built from mLSTM layers handle bytes, a boundary predictor groups them into patches, and the frozen transformer from the source model stays in the middle. Training runs in two stages, first mimicking the source model and then end to end, and the boundary predictor is deliberately non-causal so it can look one byte ahead and reproduce tokenizer boundaries. The byteified models Bolmo 7B, Bwen 8B and Blama 8B were trained on roughly 49B tokens and land near their source models on MMLU, ARC, HumanEval and GSM8K, improve 16.5 points over earlier byte-level approaches on STEM tasks, and beat their subword originals on character-level benchmarks such as CUTE and EXECUTE.

*** Take
Converting an existing model rather than pretraining a byte-level one from scratch is what makes this practical, because nobody is going to spend a frontier pretraining budget to fix tokenizer artefacts. The character-level gains are the tell: counting letters and editing strings are tasks that subword models fail for reasons that have nothing to do with capability. The flexible compression ratio is the part to watch, since it decides whether byte-level inference stays affordable.

***** Title
把语言模型改造成直接处理字节
***** TLDR
Allen AI 与合作者发表在《Nature》上的论文提出 byteification：把现有的子词语言模型改造成直接处理原始字节，而不必从头重训。由 mLSTM 层构成的本地编码器和解码器负责字节，一个边界预测器把字节分组成 patch，源模型中被冻结的 transformer 留在中间。训练分两阶段，先模仿源模型，再端到端微调；边界预测器被刻意设计为非因果的，这样它可以向前看一个字节，复现分词器的边界。改造后的 Bolmo 7B、Bwen 8B 和 Blama 8B 在约 490 亿 token 上训练，在 MMLU、ARC、HumanEval 和 GSM8K 上接近各自源模型，在 STEM 任务上比此前的字节级方法高出 16.5 个百分点，并在 CUTE、EXECUTE 等字符级基准上超过原本的子词模型。
***** Take
真正让这件事可行的是改造现有模型，而不是从零预训练一个字节级模型，因为没人会为了修补分词器的毛病去烧一笔前沿预训练预算。字符级任务的提升最能说明问题：数字母、改字符串这类失败，和模型能力本身无关。接下来要关注的是可变压缩率，它决定字节级推理的成本能否接受。
