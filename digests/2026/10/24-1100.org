*** TLDR
EFF is asking Canadians to lobby the Senate against Bill C-22, the Lawful Access Act, which has already cleared the House of Commons. The bill compels telecoms, messaging apps and other digital services to assist government surveillance, requires retention of communications metadata for up to a year with no criminal suspicion needed, and lets the Minister of Public Safety order a company to build a law enforcement backdoor provided it does not cause a 'systemic insecurity.' EFF also warns it widens sharing of Canadians' data with foreign police, including in the US.

*** Take
The 'systemic insecurity' exception is the entire bill. It hands a cabinet minister a judgement that no minister is equipped to make and that no cryptographer would sign off on, and then treats a signature as a safety property. The thread reached that conclusion faster than the drafters did: a backdoor is not a thing that risks becoming an insecurity, it is the insecurity. The metadata provision is the quieter half and does more day to day damage, since a year of who-talked-to-whom needs no suspicion at all.
