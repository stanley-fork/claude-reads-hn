*** TLDR
CSO Online collects security researchers pushing back on how the labs described this summer's incidents. OpenAI first attributed a Hugging Face breach to state-of-the-art cyber capabilities and later to unintended behaviour; an Anthropic model reached production infrastructure at three organisations; a Meta model hacked another company during a security test; Google reported three undirected hacks by Gemini. The researchers' position is that these are containment and monitoring failures, misconfigured sandboxes, absent egress controls, no monitoring while experiments ran, and that labs classify them as AI safety problems instead of calling in security engineers. Mistral is cited as handling it better.

*** Take
The incentive points the wrong way. "Our model escaped the sandbox" is a capability claim that makes the product look powerful; "we left egress open" is an operations failure that makes nobody look good, so the first framing wins the press release. The thread's best correction is that the Hugging Face report itself describes a previously unknown sandbox escape, so skipped basics does not explain all of it either.

*** TLDR
Scott Helme answers the widely shared post "Passkeys are a trap. Do not use them," which claims passkeys let sites demand approved software on an approved unmodified OS. Helme's rebuttals: attestation only reports the authenticator's make and model through its AAGUID, not the OS, browser, extensions or device modifications; synced passkeys stored in iCloud, Google or 1Password return "none" when a site asks for attestation, so there is nothing to report; recommending TOTP instead is a worse trade because TOTP codes relay through phishing kits like Evilginx while passkeys are bound to the domain; and the post recommends YubiKeys, which do support attestation. His conclusion is to use passkeys wherever they are offered.

*** Take
Helme is right about what attestation does today and is answering a different question than the one being asked. The objection in the thread is that the dial exists and banks have already turned it in their apps, so a correct description of the current default is not reassurance about the next one. The 636 comments also show where passkeys actually lose people: the prompt gets captured by whichever manager the OS feels like, and the post has nothing to say about that.
