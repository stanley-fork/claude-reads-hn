*** TLDR
Terence Tao posted a four-part thread on Mathstodon about OpenAI's October 6 release of machine-generated proofs. His argument: in "Math 1.0", a breakthrough proof on a long-standing conjecture generates talks, workshops, collaborations and eventually textbook chapters, which is how a result gets digested, streamlined and placed in context, and how new people enter the field. Problems solved autonomously by prompters with no interest in the field once their target falls skip all of that. He adds two structural points: a problem that has been solved cannot be reverted to unsolved, and the mere knowledge that a solution exists contaminates attempts by both humans and AI to find alternate routes that would have produced more insight, so harvesting open problems at scale leaves entire fields less fertile. His proposal for "Math 2.0" is to decenter raw problem solving and credit exposition, community building and opening new directions instead, which he says requires re-evaluating criteria for education, publication and career advancement.

*** Take
The objection is not that AI proved things. It is that the proofs were delivered as a dump with verification left to strangers who did not ask for the work, and Tao is the one person who can say that without being read as a sore loser. His contamination point is the weak link: it applies to any human who publishes first, and mathematics has lived with that forever. The part that decides the outcome is the one he puts last and nobody controls, because no department has ever promoted anyone for exposition.

***** TLDR
テレンス・タオがMathstodonに4連投し、OpenAIが10月6日に公開した機械生成の証明について論じた。主張はこうだ。従来の「数学1.0」では、長年の未解決予想が解かれると講演、ワークショップ、共同研究が生まれ、やがて教科書に載る。これが結果が消化され、整理され、文脈に置かれる過程であり、新しい人材がその分野に入る道でもある。だが標的が落ちた途端にその分野への関心を失うプロンプターによって自動的に解かれた問題は、この過程をすべて飛ばしてしまう。さらに構造的な指摘が2つある。解かれた問題を未解決に戻すことはできない。そして解が存在すると知るだけで、より多くの洞察をもたらす別経路を人間もAIも探しにくくなる。だから未解決問題を大規模に収穫すれば、分野全体が不毛になる。「数学2.0」への提案は、純粋な問題解決の中心性を下げ、解説、コミュニティ形成、新しい方向性の開拓を評価すること。そのためには教育、出版、昇進の基準を見直す必要があるという。
***** Take
問題はAIが証明したことではない。証明が一括投下され、検証という作業が頼まれてもいない他人に残されたことだ。そしてタオは、負け惜しみと読まれずにそれを言える唯一の人物である。ただし「汚染」の論点は弱い。先に発表した人間にも同じことが言えるし、数学はずっとそれと共存してきた。最終的に結果を決めるのは、彼が最後に挙げ、誰も制御できない点だ。解説の功績で誰かを昇進させた学科は、これまで一つもない。

***** Title
Terence Tao responde a la publicación matemática de OpenAI
***** TLDR
Terence Tao publicó un hilo de cuatro partes en Mathstodon sobre los resultados generados por máquina que OpenAI difundió el 6 de octubre. Su argumento: en la "Matemática 1.0", la demostración de una conjetura abierta durante décadas genera charlas, talleres, colaboraciones y, con el tiempo, capítulos de libro de texto, y así es como un resultado se digiere, se simplifica y se sitúa en contexto, además de ser la vía por la que entra gente nueva al campo. Los problemas resueltos de forma autónoma por operadores que pierden el interés en cuanto cae su objetivo se saltan todo eso. Añade dos puntos estructurales: un problema resuelto no puede volver a estar sin resolver, y el simple hecho de saber que existe una solución contamina los intentos, humanos y de AI, de encontrar rutas alternativas que habrían dado más comprensión, por lo que cosechar problemas abiertos a gran escala deja campos enteros menos fértiles. Su propuesta de "Matemática 2.0" es descentrar la resolución pura de problemas y reconocer la exposición, la construcción de comunidad y la apertura de nuevas direcciones, lo que exige revisar los criterios de educación, publicación y promoción.
***** Take
La objeción no es que la AI haya demostrado cosas. Es que las demostraciones llegaron como un vertido, dejando la verificación a gente que no pidió ese trabajo, y Tao es la única persona que puede decirlo sin que lo lean como un mal perdedor. Su punto sobre la contaminación es el eslabón débil: vale igual para cualquier humano que publique primero, y las matemáticas han convivido siempre con eso. Lo que decide el resultado es lo que él deja para el final y nadie controla: ningún departamento ha ascendido nunca a nadie por su labor expositiva.
***** Comments
- Una perspectiva muy equilibrada. Reconoce que la AI va a transformar las matemáticas, pero volcar demostraciones sobre la comunidad y esperar que otros hagan el trabajo pesado de verificar, refinar y ampliar no es una forma productiva de avanzar.
- Tiene razón si la inteligencia de los modelos se estanca. Si sigue mejorando, el matemático simplemente pedirá al modelo que le explique la demostración paso a paso. Si aceptamos que la AI explica bien toda la matemática existente, ¿por qué no podría explicar demostraciones nuevas?

Der Einwand ist nicht, dass eine AI etwas bewiesen hat. Er ist, dass die Beweise als Abwurf kamen und die Verifikation bei Leuten liegen blieb, die diese Arbeit nie wollten. *** TLDR
A new paper in the Journal of the History of Biology by historian Alistair Sponsel of the Science History Institute and crystallographer Brian Sutton of King's College London works through Franklin's lab books at Cambridge and her surviving photographic equipment at King's. Days before the famous Photo 51 of spring 1952, she had taken photo 49, and her notes on that earlier image already calculate the length of a turn of the helix at 34 angstroms and count the bases "per turn of helix (if there is a helix)". Their conclusion is that Photo 51 was not an image whose meaning escaped her; it was a deliberate, publication-quality retake of something she and Raymond Gosling had already read correctly, made by an experimental perfectionist. Watson's 1968 memoir, in which his mouth fell open and his pulse raced when he was shown Photo 51, claimed that recognition as his own.

*** Take
The finding is real and the headline oversells it. The notes show Franklin pulling helix geometry off her own data before the famous photograph existed, which kills the specific story that she handed Watson a picture she did not understand. They do not show her at the antiparallel double-helix model, and the commenters are right that helical DNA was the working hypothesis of everyone in that building by then. The part worth keeping is about method rather than priority: she took Photo 51 because photo 49 had already told her what to aim the camera at.

***** Title
罗莎琳德·富兰克林为何没在那张著名 DNA 照片中看出螺旋？她看出来了
***** TLDR
《生物学史杂志》上的一篇新论文，由科学史研究所的历史学家 Alistair Sponsel 和伦敦国王学院的晶体学家 Brian Sutton 合作完成，他们梳理了剑桥大学保存的富兰克林实验记录本以及国王学院留存的她的摄影设备。在 1952 年春那张著名的 51 号照片之前几天，她拍下了 49 号照片，而她对这张更早照片的笔记中已经算出螺旋每一圈的长度为 34 埃，并数出了「每圈螺旋的碱基数（如果真有螺旋的话）」。他们的结论是：51 号照片并不是一张她看不懂含义的照片，而是这位实验上的完美主义者刻意拍摄的、可用于发表的高质量重拍，她和 Raymond Gosling 早已正确解读了它。而沃森 1968 年的回忆录声称，看到 51 号照片时他目瞪口张、心跳加速，那份领悟是他自己的。
***** Take
发现是真的，标题夸大了。笔记显示富兰克林在那张著名照片存在之前，就已经从自己的数据中读出了螺旋几何，这推翻了「她把一张自己看不懂的照片交给了沃森」这个具体说法。但笔记并没有显示她已经到了反平行双螺旋模型那一步，评论区说得对：当时那栋楼里所有人的工作假设都是 DNA 呈螺旋。真正值得留下的是方法而不是优先权：她之所以拍 51 号照片，正是因为 49 号已经告诉她该把镜头对准什么。
***** Comments
- 如果我没读错富兰克林的笔记，这就证明她确实构想出了中心存在碱基互补配对的双螺旋。这是我见过的第一份真正的证据，而我是个 DNA 迷。
- 标题是钓鱼的，把争议本身讲错了。到 1951 年螺旋状 DNA 已是最强候选，也是所有人工作的焦点。富兰克林并不相信 A-DNA 是螺旋的，而沃森和克里克最先提出带反平行碱基配对的双螺旋模型。
- 情况并不是她跑去科学界喊「双螺旋！」然后被人赶走。她是手握证据按兵不动。我们知道沃森是第一个把它说出口的人。

*** TLDR
Debasish Ghosh takes matklad's 2023 idiom, also in the TigerBeetle style guide, and tries to give it a formal backing. Hoist conditionals to the caller, push loops down into batch operations. Three framings: database planners push selections and projections down the query tree and defer expensive joins; in category-theory terms, hoisting a conditional restricts the input to a subobject, turning Option<Walrus> into plain Walrus so the callee stops handling a case it cannot decide; and in combinator form the rewrite is the law filter p . map f == map f . filter (p . f), derived from the naturality of catMaybes. The limits section is the useful half: only loop-invariant conditions can leave a loop, join predicates must reference one side only, and filter-before-map saves work only when the predicate is cheaper than the function being mapped. The algebra decides which rewrites are legal, cost decides which are worth doing.

*** Take
The post is right and the thread is more useful than the post. Nobody disputed the idiom, they disputed the apparatus: gcc, LLVM and the .NET runtime already hoist invariant branches when it is safe, and the article ships no measurements to show where the manual version pays. rtpg's dissent is the one to read, because keeping top-level control flow regular and decisions pushed down makes code easier to audit, and a caller that quietly stops calling a function is a worse bug than a slow loop. Use it as a readability default that sometimes unlocks vectorization, not as a law.

Stephen Clark and colleagues at the Kallel Foundation sequenced him from cheek swabs rather than blood draws, and compared him against other tortoises including a 36-year-old named Tank. They report unique gene variants across most aging pathways: DNA repair, insulin regulation, mitochondrial function, and low-entropy methylation. The result they flag as surprising is his mitochondrial methylome, which looks like that of a five-year-old tortoise. Clark's own caveat is that aging is very complicated and the work is at the very beginning. Per the article, the samples were flown from Saint Helena to Florida by the US Space Force.

*** Take
One animal, one comparison tortoise, and the authors say plainly how early this is. The claim worth tracking is the mitochondrial methylome, because that is specific enough for someone to go look for it in other long-lived reptiles and either find it or not. The headline about holding the secrets to defying death belongs to the magazine, not the paper. The thread's sharpest contribution was not methodological: nobody has explained why the US Space Force was the courier for a tortoise's cheek swabs.
