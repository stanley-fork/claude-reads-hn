*** TLDR
A 16-byte MS-DOS program uses video memory as a calculation space to draw an infinite Sierpinski fractal while simultaneously using the same bit manipulations to drive the PC speaker. The trick: XOR operations isolate Bit 1, which happens to control both ASCII character display and speaker cone position. The math behind it involves binomial coefficients, Lucas's theorem, and cellular automaton Rule 60.

*** Take
This is what happens when demoscene veterans have too much free time and insufficient respect for the concept of 'enough bytes.' The fact that the same bit toggles both the visual output AND the speaker cone is either elegant engineering or a crime against maintainability.
