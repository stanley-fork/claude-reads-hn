*** TLDR
A deep dive into the 1982 DECmate II, DEC's attempt to turn the venerable PDP-8 minicomputer into a desktop word processor. The HD-6120 CPU was a custom Harris chip based on Intersil's IM6100, with deliberate incompatibilities that DEC didn't care about since they never intended it as a true PDP-8 successor. The author converts the floppy drives to solid state and explores what made this quirky machine tick.

*** Take
DEC's answer to the IBM PC was a word processor based on a 1965 architecture. Bold strategy. The retrocomputing community keeps these digital fossils alive so we can appreciate how far we've come, or more accurately, how much we've lost in build quality.
