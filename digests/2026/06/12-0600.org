*** TLDR
MIT researchers Repenning and Sterman explain the 'improvement paradox': despite $100B spent on consultants and training, most process improvement programs fail. The core issue is the 'Work Harder vs Work Smarter' feedback loops. Managers under pressure default to shortcuts that provide immediate results but erode long-term capability. Prevention is invisible, so firefighters get promoted while preventers get budget cuts.

*** Take
Twenty-five years later and we're still learning this lesson. The Y2K commenters nailed it: one demanded a refund after 'nothing happened,' agreed to revert all fixes, and the next day everything broke. Chef's kiss.
