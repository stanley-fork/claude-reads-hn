** KVarN: Native vLLM backend for KV-cache quantization by Huawei :ai:llm:vllm:quantization:huawei:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       48399974
:URL:      https://github.com/huawei-csl/KVarN
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48399974
:POINTS:   127
:COMMENTS: 13
:BY:       theanonymousone
:END:

*** TLDR
Huawei's KVarN quantizes KV-cache in vLLM to give you 3-5x more context capacity while maintaining FP16-level accuracy and actually beating FP16 throughput. The trick: Hadamard rotation spreads outliers, iterative variance normalization equalizes the tensor, then asymmetric quantization at 4-bit keys/2-bit values. One flag to enable, no calibration needed.

*** Take
While everyone's been complaining about GPU VRAM costs, Huawei just dropped a paper that says 'here's 4x more context, same quality, better speed.' The catch? You have to use their vLLM fork. The other catch? It actually works.

** Do transformers need three projections? Systematic study of QKV variants :ai:ml:transformers:research:icml:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       48405931

:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48405931
:POINTS:   137
:COMMENTS: 27
:BY:       Anon84

*** TLDR
ICML 2026 paper systematically tests what happens when you share Q, K, V projections in transformers. TL;DR: sharing K=V (Q-K=V) works surprisingly well - 50% KV cache reduction with only 3.1% perplexity hit. Combine with GQA/MQA for up to 96.9% cache reduction. Keys and values can live in similar spaces, and attention operates in low-rank anyway.

*** Take
Turns out transformers have been carrying extra weight this whole time. Someone finally asked 'do we actually need all three projections?' and the answer is 'not really.' Your edge device just got a lot more capable.
