*** TLDR
OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Sol at $2 per million input tokens, $0.10 for cached input, and $10 per million output. The announcement claims it matches GPT-6 Astra on the DeepSWE v1.1 coding benchmark at roughly one fifth the cost, beats GPT-6 Sol there by 6.4 points, scores 2.2 points above Opus 5.5 on AutomationBench, lands within 2.1 points of Astra on OSWorld 2.0 computer use, more than doubles GPT-6 Sol on scientific research, and cuts factual error rates from 11.4% to 7.7% at low reasoning effort. *** Take
Read the benchmark table again: nearly every line is a price comparison, not a capability claim. Matches Astra at one fifth the cost. Beats Opus 5.5 at less than half. That is the pitch of a company competing on margin, not on the frontier. And shipping a 6.1 within days of 6 is an admission that 6 Sol went out before it was ready.

** How Delhi cut electricity loss from 50 to 5 percent :energy:grid:india:infrastructure:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       49892245
:URL:      https://spectrum.ieee.org/delhi-electricity-loss
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49892245
:POINTS:   434
:COMMENTS: 251
:BY:       rbanffy
:END:

*** TLDR
IEEE Spectrum traces 25 years of work on Delhi's grid. In 2002 the Delhi Vidyut Board was dissolved and distribution handed to Tata Power in the north and BSES/Reliance in the south and east; India's Electricity Act of 2003 unbundled generation, transmission and distribution and created machinery for prosecuting theft. Tata went from 53.5% losses to about 6.3% by 2023, BSES from 51.5-63.1% to low single digits. The interventions were mostly unglamorous: SCADA for real-time visibility, transformer replacement that dropped failure rates from 11% to under 1%, hundreds of capacitor banks, insulated cable in place of bare wire, electronic meters in 2003, automated meters in 2004, smart meters from 2017, and handheld digital meter reading with 24-hour payment kiosks. In pockets where losses hit 83 percent, Tata and later BSES hired local women as bill collectors and paired that with water supply and literacy programs. Reliability went from roughly 70% in 2002 to over 99.9% in 2026.

*** Take
The meters are not the story. Delhi's losses were overwhelmingly theft and unbilled consumption, and the thing that worked in the worst neighborhoods was paying residents to collect from their neighbors instead of sending enforcement in. That is a political fix wearing an engineering costume, which is exactly why a utility elsewhere cannot buy the same SCADA license and expect the same curve.
