*** TLDR
The Second Section of Spain's Intellectual Property Commission, part of the Ministry of Culture, ordered ISPs to block archive.today and its mirrors. No court ruling was involved: the commission acts on a complaint, reviews it internally, and issues an administrative order. The stated grounds are that the domains were illegally facilitating access to copyright-protected content. Spaniards in the thread report the block is patchy in practice.

*** Take
The interesting part is not the block, it is the procedure. A ministry body can now take a site off the Spanish internet on a copyright complaint without a judge reading the file. Spain already showed what that pipeline does at scale with the LaLiga football blocks that took out Cloudflare IP ranges. Archive.today is a link-rot backstop for journalists and researchers, which is exactly the kind of collateral an expedited process is bad at noticing.

*** TLDR
Jan-Olaf Meynecke and Andrew Mulville of Griffith University documented a female humpback whale and her escort attending a stillborn calf at Palm Beach reef on Australia's Gold Coast on 17 July 2025. Observed from a private vessel between 8 and 10 AM and filmed with an underwater GoPro, the female touched the calf with her head and pectoral fin and held position eye to eye with it for stretches of at least a minute. The paper, in Discovery Animal, calls it the first detailed documented occurrence of this behaviour in the species and notes that post-mortem attendance in baleen whales is poorly understood because it is so rarely witnessed.

*** Take
The claim here is narrower than the headline. The researchers documented sustained post-mortem attendance and say it may be grief-related; they do not assert that whales grieve the way people do. That distinction is worth holding onto, because it is what makes the observation usable. Baleen whales are hard to watch closely, and a two-hour underwater record of one animal's behaviour toward a dead calf is a data point the literature did not have.

** UTF-8000: Unlimited UTF-8 :unicode:encoding:programming:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       49772677
:URL:      https://utf-8000.jb2170.com
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49772677
:POINTS:   73
:COMMENTS: 46
:BY:       vismit2000
:END:

*** TLDR
UTF-8000 extends UTF-8 to arbitrarily large codepoints. It splits the high bits of a start byte into two jobs: two synchronisation bits that mark first bytes versus continuation bytes, and a unary run of start bits that encodes the length. Past seven bytes the start bits roll over into continuation bytes, which then carry more unary bits until the terminating zero. An n-byte unit holds 5n+1 content bits for n greater than 2. The author claims self-synchronisation, strcmp ordering and overlong-encoding checks all survive. Ken Thompson pointed out the cost: beyond seven bytes you cannot know a unit's length without reading further. The author expects no adoption.

*** Take
This is a clean piece of encoding design that nobody needs, and the author says so himself. *** TLDR
The Digital Antiquarian traces what happened to Lemmings after the 1991 original. Lemmings 2: The Tribes (1993) added 60 abilities across 12 tribes, impressed veterans, lost newcomers, and shipped on 8 platforms against the original's 24. All New World of Lemmings (1994) added tedious item collection and a tone that fit nothing. Lemmings 3D (1995) converted a 2D puzzle into an argument with a camera. Only Lemmings Revolution (2000), built around rotating a cylindrical level, worked. The argument: DMA Design wanted to build harder and more ambitious games while the franchise's value was its accessibility, and Psygnosis under Sony pushed it further off course, including a failed Children's Television Workshop tie-in.

*** Take
The pattern is not nostalgia, it is a repeatable failure mode. The team that ships a mass-market hit is usually bored by the thing that made it a hit, and every sequel gets designed for the people who already finished the first one. Lemmings 2 shipping to a third as many platforms is the number that settles it: the sequel was aimed at a smaller audience before anyone played it. Revolution succeeded by changing the geometry instead of the difficulty.
