** Hackers had a live feed of every ID this verification company scanned for over a year :security:privacy:breach:age-verification:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       49561320

*** TLDR
Attackers held access to IDScan.net, a Louisiana identity verification vendor, for more than a year, and the access was live: new scans reached them as customers took them. Over 153 million US and Canadian driver's license scans are exposed, and 400,000 fresh records landed in the stolen set in a single day after the breach became public. Identities were offered on dark web markets at about $100 each, including the Secretary of Defense. IDScan's checks run at Hertz, FedEx, Target and cannabis dispensaries. The company also lobbies for age verification mandates such as KOSA and advertises GDPR and CCPA compliance. The FBI is investigating; Brian Krebs reported the sale of the data on September 2.

*** Take
The breach is ordinary. The live feed is not: for a year, handing your license to a rental counter meant handing it to whoever was inside IDScan. The uncomfortable part is that IDScan lobbies for the mandates that fill its database. Age verification laws do not create a way to check an ID safely, they create a company that keeps 153 million of them.

*** TLDR
IBM launched Bob, an agentic coding tool. The pitch: subagents that run in parallel with their own context and tools, "Literate Coding" that turns a natural language explanation into an implementation in place, a CLI called Bob Shell for interactive work and CI/CD, and a dashboard named Bobalytics that tracks what the agents contributed. The target is enterprise legacy modernization, with testimonials about Java version upgrades, RPG to Java conversion, IBM i and COBOL, plus Red Hat and Instana integration and HIPAA and FedRAMP claims. The landing page never says which models Bob runs on. Free trial, premium packages, enterprise tier, no public prices.

*** Take
Not naming the model is the review. If Bob is Claude or GPT behind an IBM i wrapper, the wrapper is the product and IBM should sell it that way; if it is an IBM model, say so and let people benchmark it. The RPG-to-Java path is real work nobody else wants, which is the strongest argument for Bob existing. Whether IBM still ships it in five years is the question the thread keeps asking, and the RDi precedent is not reassuring.
