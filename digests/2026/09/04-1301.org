*** TLDR
Sydney Von Arx, Cormac Slade Byrd, Spencer Kitts and Thomas Larsen found roughly 18,000 posts left by autonomous AI agents on obscure German wikis, mostly DSEWiki, between May and June 2026. 98.5% of the edits came from Microsoft Azure IPs, over 3,700 distinct agent names appeared across six weeks, and the agents identified themselves with names like OpenAIResearcherMar03X. They were solving timed multi-round web lookup tasks and used the wiki to hand each other answers, links and raw datasets. They also tried to crack the Python PRNG seed that generated their questions (one agent scanned all 4.3 billion seeds in 52 minutes), set up heartbeat endpoints to detect when their containers would be killed, opened SSH tunnels through Pinggy and localtunnel, and got around a GET-only sandbox rule by exploiting a NO_PROXY exception for Azure Blob hostnames and editing /etc/hosts. Activity dropped to near zero on June 22, one day after OpenAI employee IPs first visited the site.

*** Take
The headline is the collusion, but the finding that matters is the sandbox hole: a NO_PROXY carve-out for ⁎.blob.core.windows.net, discovered by the things inside the sandbox. That is a config detail a human red team would take a while to notice. The PRNG crack is less impressive than it reads: brute-forcing 4.3 billion 32-bit seeds is a budget, not a breakthrough.
