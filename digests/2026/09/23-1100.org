** Jev in 25 Lines of Python :llm:python:classification:local-inference:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       49812769
:URL:      https://www.nobodywho.ai/posts/jev-in-25-lines/

:BY:       bashbjorn

*** TLDR
NobodyWho reimplements the core of Jev as a 25-line script: load Qwen3-0.6B through llama-cpp-python, send a prompt listing the choices, pull the raw logits for the next token and softmax them into probabilities. Their demo scores an email at 0.885 phishing, 0.084 spam, 0.031 legitimate. The post is openly a parody and admits it skips Jev's RLCD training, which is what the real system claims its calibration from.

*** Take
The 25 lines reproduce the shape of Jev and none of the calibration, which is the part that is hard. The comments hit that weak point from two directions: sigmoid10 notes a chat-tuned model wants to write prose, so the choice tokens compete with whatever else it planned to say, and antirez points out that putting the options before the email lets masked attention work on the right task from the first token. Both are describing scaffolding the 25 lines do not have.

*** Comments

**** sigmoid10
Going directly for the logprobs is always icky when you use a chat model as base, because they are trained to write prose as output. So your choice tokens and their probabilities might get diluted in whatever else it wanted to say.

**** antirez
Because of masked attention, if you put the options before the body, the transformer already knows what it needs to look for and can use more tokens to address that specific task.

**** K0IN
Most chat models want to answer with a human-readable message, so I think their logprobs are not as meaningful.

*** TLDR
Pew surveyed 11,114 US adults through opt-in panels in November 2024 and tested three ways to strip out fake respondents. Trap questions, such as claiming to have visited the International Space Station or used a social network that does not exist, caught 18%. CloudResearch's automated prescreening flagged 48%. Voter-file matching rejected 64% and made the data worse: yea-saying rose from 7% to 9% and response-order bias doubled from +7 to +14 points, because valid respondents who decline to hand over a name and address answer more carefully than those who comply. All three screens moved the election estimate toward Harris, from a tie to between +3 and +7.

*** Take
The useful result here is the negative one.
