** Telstra outage: The night a network decided the year was 2006 :networking:ntp:outage:telstra:infrastructure:

:ID:       49748957

*** TLDR
On 8 July 2026 a GPS receiver in Melbourne came back from maintenance with firmware old enough to hit a week-number rollover, so it believed the year was 2006. It advertised itself as stratum 1, the top rank in the NTP hierarchy, and every client below it accepted the date. NTP's outlier detection should have voted it down, but Melbourne's backup stratum 2 server had been decommissioned years earlier, so nothing independent disagreed. The bad timestamp propagated, downstream servers echoed it back as confirmation, and voice, SMS, emergency calling, trains, payment terminals, ticketing and EV chargers went down. Telstra's TAP report concluded the protocol worked and the architecture did not: five years of topology changes and skipped firmware updates had quietly removed every source of disagreement.

*** Take
The interesting failure is not the rollover bug, it is that someone deleted the only server whose job was to disagree. Redundancy that exists to contradict a peer looks like idle duplication on a cost review, right up to the morning it is the only thing standing between you and a nationwide outage. Telstra spent five years making the network tidier and each individual change was defensible.

*** TLDR
Alibaba released Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, a natively omnimodal model taking text, image, audio and video with a 1M-token context and up to an hour of continuous audio-visual input. It claims 71.0 vs Gemini 3.8 Flash's 58.9 on WildClawBench-MM while losing OmniVideoBench 63.4 to 65.2, and its headline number is speaker diarization error of 3.35 against Gemini's 88.1. The realtime variant reports 591-981ms time to first token and 0.154 real-time factor for audio generation. Per-hour API pricing is down 98% for audio and 93% for audio-visual versus Qwen3.5. No parameter count, no training details, and despite the Qwen brand this one is closed: API only through Alibaba's platform, no weights.

*** Take
The diarization gap of 3.35 against 88.1 is too large to be a straight capability comparison. A number like that usually means the two models were measured under different output formats or Gemini was run in a mode it was not built for, and the blog gives you nothing to check it against. The credible part is the pricing: an order-of-magnitude cut on audio hours is a real product decision, and it is aimed squarely at the one modality Gemini was winning on. Worth noting that the lab with the open-weights reputation shipped this one closed.

Thomas Ptacek argues you should use an LLM as a copyeditor, not a ghostwriter. *** Take

A model that tells you the draft is strong removes the discomfort that makes you rewrite, and that is a bigger loss than any borrowed phrase. *** TLDR
Roughly a thousand Ancient Greek words have no Proto-Indo-European etymology, and the article presents them as borrowings from whatever was spoken in the Aegean before Greek speakers arrived. The evidence is threefold: vocabulary clustered in flora, fauna and place names such as ἐλαία (olive), λαβύρινθος (labyrinth) and Ἀθῆναι (Athens); sound sequences like /dn/ and /ps/ that no Indo-European sound law produces; and recurring suffixes, -nth in Corinth and hyacinth, -ss in Knossos, -mn in Lemnos. Variation like Odysseus against Ulysses suggests Greek speakers were approximating sounds their own phonology did not have. The article's source is Robert Beekes' etymological dictionary, which tags 1,106 words as Pre-Greek, and it concedes the main objection: Beekes treats every non-Greek word as belonging to one substrate language.

*** Take
The suffix clusters are the real evidence and they survive the critique. The single-language framing does not. Beekes' method produces a substrate by definition, because anything he cannot derive from Indo-European becomes Pre-Greek, and the Aegean in the second millennium BC plainly held more than one non-Greek language. What the data supports is that Greek borrowed heavily from neighbours it could not fully pronounce. Which neighbours, and how many, is the part nobody can currently answer.
