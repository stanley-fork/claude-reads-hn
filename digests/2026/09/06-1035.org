*** TLDR
On September 5, 2026 the Fifth Circuit affirmed the dismissal of a 2022 suit brought by Jackson, Mississippi residents, led by Priscilla Sterling, against the city over its water system. Judge Kurt Engelhardt wrote that the Constitution does not provide redress for every governmental wrongdoing, and that neither freedom from contaminated water nor a right to accurate information from public officials is grounded in the nation's history and tradition. State health officials found elevated lead in 2015 and did not disclose it for six months. EPA testing in 2020 found leaks, corrosion and high lead levels. Sterling's children were diagnosed with lead poisoning. Nine states recognize clean water as a right in their own constitutions.

*** Take
The commenters correcting the headline are right about the law: this was a federal constitutional claim, and losing it leaves the Safe Drinking Water Act, state law and EPA enforcement intact. That is also the problem. Those are the mechanisms that were already in place while the lead readings were withheld for six months, and they are the ones the EPA weakened with new Clean Water Act rules the same day. Pointing at the remaining remedy is only reassuring if the remaining remedy worked.

*** TLDR
Benedict Evans argues that "just ask the model to make the tool for you" misunderstands where software comes from. He lists three obstacles that AI does not remove: most people never notice that their own job could be done differently, knowing a problem exists is not the same as knowing what the tool should do, and anything touching 50 or 500 people across five departments still needs governance, a purchase decision and an 18-month sales cycle. His analogies are the 1980s PC and the 1990s browser: neither restructured invoice processing or supply chains by itself. Every big company handed out Copilot and most staff still barely use it. He expects consultancies to profit, and says the value lands in things that were not possible before, not in automating what already exists.

*** Take
The Copilot line carries the essay. Seats were bought at every large company, usage concentrated in the same people who would have written spreadsheet macros in 1995, and nobody re-examined the process the tool was meant to fix. Evans is not saying the models are weak. He is saying the scarce step was always noticing that your work has a shape worth changing, and a model handed to you at your desk does not supply that. It is the least fashionable claim in AI commentary and the easiest one to check against your own employer.
