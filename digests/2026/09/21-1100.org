** Kev: Tiny Jev-like family of decision models built on top of Qwen3.5 :ai:llm:open-source:classification:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       49783999
:URL:      https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/tree/main
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49783999
:POINTS:   125
:COMMENTS: 56
:BY:       tosh
:END:

*** TLDR
Kev is three models at 0.8B, 4B and 9B, LoRA fine-tunes of Qwen3.5 bases that answer structured questions about a text input: yes/no, multiple choice and rating questions, several per request, each question answered in isolation. Training was about 10,000 public examples plus 2,576 generated from policy rules, rank-16 LoRA, cross-entropy loss. Apache-2.0, runs locally. The README points at support ticket routing, escalation detection and sentiment, and says a short fine-tune on a few hundred labelled examples helps more than any prompt change.

*** Take
The useful claim here is not that Kev matches Jev, because it does not: benchmarks posted in the thread show only marginal gains over the plain Qwen base. It is that most of this work was always classification, and the industry spent two years paying frontier-model prices per ticket to do it. A 4B model you can fine-tune on 300 labelled rows is the boring correct answer.

Both camps are reading the file that decided it.
