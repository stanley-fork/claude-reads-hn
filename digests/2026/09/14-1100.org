*** TLDR
Gianni Rosato, who works on AVIF encoding tooling, argues browsers should not ship JPEG XL. His numbers: AVIF wins on CVVDP, MS-SSIM and SSIMULACRA2 across the fidelity range, helped by libaom and SVT-AV1 perceptual tuning derived from subjective trials; lossless JXL is only about 11.9% smaller than lossless WebP on his test set; WebP decodes more than 10x faster than jxl-rs; a crafted 1,918-byte JXL file takes 17.43 seconds of user CPU to decode; recompressed JPEGs decode about 33% slower; and progressive AVIF shows a usable image at 2-3% of the bytes. He grants JXL is strong for professional and archival work, and says the web does not need a second new codec, least of all a slower-decoding one. Revisit: the thread has more than doubled since our last edition.

*** Take
The decode measurements are the real contribution, and a 1,918-byte file that pins a core for 17 seconds is a serious argument against putting the format in a browser. The rest is weaker. A 12% lossless win over WebP gets dismissed as too small while AVIF's metric wins over JXL are treated as decisive, and the first substantive reply says the progressive AVIF claim is wrong, that libavif renders ordered tiles incrementally rather than truly decoding progressively. 'The web needs no more codecs' is also what Chrome said in 2022, which is why JXL has had four thin years of implementation work to be benchmarked against.

*** TLDR
Calif.io says an ordinary Android app running as untrusted_app, declaring no permissions at all, can reach root on current flagships from Samsung, Xiaomi, Oppo, OnePlus and Realme. The chain has two stages: abuse a logic flaw in an OEM IPC endpoint to move into an OEM-owned process that SELinux policy allows to talk to the vendor's kernel drivers, then exploit a page use-after-free in those drivers to get read/write access to freed physical memory. Because it is data-only, slab hardening, KASLR and CFI do not stop it. Claimed coverage includes at least the Galaxy S23 through S26 and recent Z series, a large share of Xiaomi mid-range and flagship devices, and recent Oppo, OnePlus and Realme flagships; tested on Galaxy S26 and S26 Ultra, Xiaomi 17, Oppo Find X9 Ultra and OnePlus Ace 6 Ultra, with the bootloader locked and verified boot still reporting green. This is part 1: no CVEs, no per-OEM chains and no patch status yet.

*** Take
Every bug in this chain sits in vendor code, which is the part Google cannot patch for you. Samsung, Xiaomi and Oppo each ship their own kernel drivers plus an IPC surface wide enough to reach them, and that added layer is where the whole exploit lives. Posting the strategy without CVEs or device details is defensible for a part 1, but it also means no reader can check which security patch level, if any, closes this on their phone.
