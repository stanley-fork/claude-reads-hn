*** TLDR
F-Droid rewrote its Android client in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose across 14 test releases, its largest update in ten years. Navigation collapses to three tabs: Discover, Search, My Apps. Search now covers descriptions, categories and translated text with better Chinese, Japanese and Korean handling, and games split into 17 genres. The Privileged Extension is not supported: 2.0 ignores FPE even when installed and instead uses Android's session installer plus the pre-approval API for background updates. Pull-to-refresh, the panic app-wipe feature and Android 6 support are gone, and Tor auto-detection folds into generic proxy settings. Rollout runs over the coming weeks. The site header points at keepandroidopen.org over Google's changes to how apps get installed.

*** Take
Dropping the Privileged Extension is the real news and it gets one paragraph. FPE was what let F-Droid install and update apps without a per-app confirmation tap on rooted or custom-ROM devices; that path now runs through Android's own installer. So F-Droid's independence from Google's install flow gets thinner in the same release whose homepage banner warns that Google is closing that flow. The Compose rewrite is fine work. The commenters saying the new UI draws no visible boundary between tappable and not are also right, and those two things are unrelated.

*** TLDR
UK Apple users now get two different levels of encryption on identical hardware and identical plans. The cause is a secret Technical Capability Notice issued in January 2025 under the Investigatory Powers Act 2016, demanding Apple be able to decrypt iCloud data protected by Advanced Data Protection, first for users worldwide and then narrowed to UK residents after US pressure in August 2025. Apple declined to build the capability. On 21 February 2025 it stopped letting new UK users turn ADP on, while anyone who had already enabled it kept it. ADP raises the number of end-to-end encrypted iCloud categories from 14 to 23, so UK users without it lose end-to-end encryption on iCloud Backup, Photos, Notes and Drive. The author calls Apple's move the least bad option and directs the blame at the secrecy of the notices.

*** Take
Apple took the option that cost Apple the least, and the resulting security gap landed entirely on whoever signed up late. That is a defensible business decision and it still leaves millions of people with worse encryption than the person next to them for a reason nobody was allowed to tell them. The notice is the part worth being angry about: a secret order whose existence Apple could not confirm, whose only visible effect was a settings toggle that quietly stopped appearing.

*** TLDR
A post asking why the liver regrows when almost nothing else in the body does. Give away half of it and it returns to full size and function in a few months, and it heals injury without scarring. The proposed answer is a three-way tradeoff between regeneration, cancer risk and energy cost. Organs that interface with the outside world get regenerative capacity and pay for it in malignancy, which is why skin and colon both regenerate and top the cancer statistics, while highly structured organs like the brain and heart give up repair to stay stable. The liver sits downstream of the digestive tract, handling toxins, bacteria and parasites at high concentration with detox chemistry that damages itself, so it needs the capacity.
