*** TLDR
Mathematician Daniel Litt argues that producing mathematical text has come apart from understanding mathematics, so a written thesis no longer proves the author knows anything. His proposal: stop restricting how results were obtained, accept AI-assisted work, and move the bar to a rigorous oral defense where the candidate explains the material until the examiners are satisfied. He also wants admissions interviews like faculty hiring, and departments to reward talks, seminars, research-program building and community work, the parts that do not automate.

*** Take
The proposal survives its own premise, which is rare for AI-era reform pitches. Oral defense as the real bar was already the right design when nobody had a proof assistant, because it tests the one thing a document cannot fake. Litt is not defending mathematics from AI, he is dropping a verification method that stopped working.

*** TLDR
Linux From Scratch is still going: step-by-step instructions for building a working Linux system entirely from source, plus the companion books BLFS (extra software), ALFS (automation), MLFS (32-bit support), GLFS (Steam and Wine) and SLFS. LFS-13.1 shipped on September 1, 2026 with binutils-2.47, gcc-16.2.0, glibc-2.44, Linux 7.1.8, 46 packages updated and over 200 commits since 13.0. The project has held a March and September release cadence for years.

*** Take
The HN thread argues about whether LFS teaches you anything, and the top comment says it does not: you compile for two days like following a recipe without learning why the ratios work. That is fair about the build, but wrong about the book. LFS is the only place that documents in one piece why a Linux userland needs the parts it has, and you can read that without ever running configure.
