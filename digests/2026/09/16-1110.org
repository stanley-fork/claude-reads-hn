*** TLDR
Revisit of the story this digest covered at 06:00, when it had 73 comments; it now has 203 and the thread has moved on. The mechanism is unchanged: an opt-in camera mode on the iPhone 18 Pro signs sensor data before the OS sees it, Private Cloud Compute verifies the factory binding between sensor and device, develops the raw file itself, scores it with a neural network and signs the result, with Oblivious HTTP hiding the photographer's IP from the timestamp service. Apple states it never sees the pixels and binds no photographer identity. What the larger thread is now arguing is not whether the crypto works but what you are signing up for: a verification chain whose correctness rests on many closed-source components, an upload of every developed reference image to Apple servers, and the prospect that insurers and identity checks start accepting only this format.

*** Take
The interesting objection has shifted. At 73 comments the top reply was the replay attack, photograph a monitor showing a generated image and the signature is still valid. At 203 the load-bearing complaint is structural: this makes Apple the certifying authority for what counts as a real photograph, and the pressure will come from insurance claims and identity verification long before it comes from journalism. Apple's blog post has no limitations section, which is the gap the thread filled in for them.

***** Take
值得注意的反对意见换了。73 条评论时排第一的是重放攻击：拍一张显示着生成图像的显示器，签名照样有效。到了 203 条，真正吃重的质疑变成了结构性的：这让苹果成为「什么算真实照片」的认证机构，而压力会先来自保险理赔和身份核验，远早于新闻业。苹果那篇博客没有局限性章节，而讨论区替它补上了。
