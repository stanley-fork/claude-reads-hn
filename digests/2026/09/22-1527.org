** OpenAI GPT-6 Astra breaks Enigma message that has resisted solution since 2005 :cryptography:ai:history:wwii:
:PROPERTIES:

*** TLDR
MVUEH is an 82-letter German Army Enigma message sent 10 July 1941 to the SS-Totenkopf Division's logistics command, unsolved since it was published in 2005. Carter Leffer pointed GPT-6 Astra at a batch of unbroken messages; it selected MVUEH, wrote its own Enigma simulator and Bombe in Python and C++, and found the key using the repeated place name ROSENOW as a crib. Two things had made it hard: the message used wheel order 253 while the rest of that day's traffic used 512, and the left rotor turned over at letter 72, which breaks standard crib attacks. The plaintext asks for a route of march and demands an immediate radio reply. Frode Weierud of Crypto Cellar Research validated the solution.

*** Take
The crib, the wheel-order search and the rotor turnover are textbook cryptanalysis, all of it in the literature for eighty years. What Astra actually supplied was the patience to grind through a message everyone else had triaged away as not worth the compute. That is a genuine contribution. It is not the one the headline is selling.

***** Comments
