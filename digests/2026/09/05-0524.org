** RSA-260 Factorized :cryptography:math:security:
:PROPERTIES:

:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49546284

*** TLDR
Eric Lu, an engineer at Cognition, announced on September 3 that he factored RSA-260, an 862-bit challenge number that had stood since 1991, into two 130-digit primes. The factors are verifiable and are already on Wikipedia. As of September 4 he has published no algorithm, software, hardware or running time. The lilting.ch writeup estimates that a standard general number field sieve run would need roughly 7,000 core-years, about 2.6x the 2,700 core-years RSA-250 took in 2020, and that RSA-1024 would need around 78x more work than RSA-260 while RSA-2048 would need about 9x10^10 times more.

*** Take
This is a result and a riddle at the same time. Anyone can multiply the two primes and confirm the answer; nobody can confirm whether it came from a large rented cluster or from something new. Until Lu publishes the method, the supported reading is that someone spent a lot of compute, and the interesting reading, an algorithmic improvement, has no evidence behind it. Note what did not change: RSA-2048 is still roughly ten orders of magnitude out of reach, so nothing here touches keys anyone is currently using.

Statichost.eu is static site hosting run by Eric Selin out of Stockholm with one rule: every layer, from git deploy to CDN, runs on European infrastructure owned by European companies. The site puts it as "No AWS, no Cloudflare, no exceptions." It deploys from GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Forgejo, SourceHut and Azure DevOps, builds Hugo, Jekyll, Astro, Next.js, Gatsby, Nuxt, Eleventy and Zola, and does custom domains with free SSL, webhook rebuilds and instant rollback. *** Take
The sovereignty claim is the product, and it is a real one. Most hosting sold as European is a reseller layer over AWS Frankfurt, and refusing Cloudflare specifically is the part that costs money. The two objections in the thread are the ones that decide whether people stay: git-only deploys with no rsync or sftp path, and 9 EUR a month to serve static files when the same money buys a European VPS. Both are fixable. The design criticism in the thread is also fair and cheaper to fix than either.
