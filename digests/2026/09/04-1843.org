*** TLDR
Productrise tracked more than 2 million product listings across more than 100,000 result pages and AI Mode responses in the US and UK between August 9 and 31, 2026. When the same product appeared in both surfaces for the same query on the same day, the lead offer in AI Mode averaged 21.6 percent more expensive. Across all listings the median was $149 in AI Mode against $100 in the shopping results. Only 1.28 percent of traditional-search products showed up in AI Mode at all; where prices differed, which was 38.1 percent of matches, AI Mode was the pricier side 68.4 percent of the time, and the seller was different 49.6 percent of the time.

*** Take
The top comment does the real damage: the baseline here is Google's shopping carousel, which sorts by price, while AI Mode draws on ordinary web results that mostly land on manufacturer pages at list price. Comparing a price-sorted widget against a not-price-sorted one produces exactly this gap. The finding that survives the objection is the 1.28 percent overlap. AI Mode is not summarizing the merchant index Google already ranks, it is pulling from a different one, and nobody outside Google can see how that one is built.
