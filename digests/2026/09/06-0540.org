*** TLDR
Zack Polizzi launched Cloud in a Bottle, an AGPL-3.0 personal cloud. An Ubuntu host runs a web dashboard that routes HTTPS to rootless hardened containers, one login authenticates you across every app, and a permission framework modeled on mobile OS APIs governs what apps can read from each other. Three deployment paths: your own hardware, a rented VPS, or Imbue's managed hosting with $10 of free credit. The app catalog is curated and, in the author's words, still pretty small.

*** Take
The container plumbing is the easy half. The thread found the hard half within the hour: a routable public IP your ISP will actually allow, a domain, and certificates that keep renewing. It also found 11 promotional issues filed on unrelated GitHub repos by an account traced back to the project's top contributor, with no disclosure. Polizzi acknowledged it, called it a mistake, and said it stops.

*** TLDR
Bryan Cantrill argues that readers now recognize LLM prose on sight and quit reading. He cites a developer survey by Cynthia Dunlop: 78% stop reading immediately once they detect AI involvement, 71% then avoid that author entirely, and 98% prefer imperfect human writing to polished machine output. He praises Pangram Labs' detector and says Oxide now requires public writing to pass it. His prescription: write the piece yourself and use the LLM as an editor, or skip the pretense and publish your prompt.

*** Take
Cantrill's claim is narrower than the argument it started. He is not saying detection is sound enough for punishment, he is saying readers who bounce do not owe anyone a confidence interval. That distinction matters because the strongest reply in the thread is about the other use: Pangram markets itself for catching students, and by its own benchmarks it is not at 100%. In the comments Cantrill names what set him off, an LLM-authored guest post on the Rust Foundation blog.

*** TLDR
Terence Tao's 2014 post is back on the front page because of Twitter rumors that Anthropic solved the Navier-Stokes Millennium problem. Tao builds an averaged version of 3D Navier-Stokes, using spatial rotations and Fourier multipliers on the nonlinear term, that keeps the energy identity and every standard function-space estimate, then proves its solutions blow up in finite time.
