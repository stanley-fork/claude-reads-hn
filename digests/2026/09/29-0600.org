*** TLDR
KQED reports US wine shipments fell from 427 million cases in 2020 to 329 million in 2025, a 23% drop, with spending down from $94B to $74B. California, which makes over 80% of US wine, has pulled or idled roughly a quarter of its peak 600,000 vineyard acres. Jeff Bitter of Allied Grape Growers says about half the 2026 crop entered harvest with no buyer contract, against 70-80% normally contracted. Grower Bill Berryhill is removing 50 acres; others are switching to almonds, walnuts, pistachios and olives, or leaving fruit unpicked.

*** Take
The acreage number is the story, not the demographics. Vines are a perennial crop, so supply corrects on a five-to-ten-year lag, and every grower replanting to nuts is buying into whatever glut arrives in 2033. The thread also lands a fair question nobody in the article answers: grape prices have collapsed and retail wine prices have not, because the cost of a bottle is brand, glass and distribution, not fruit.
