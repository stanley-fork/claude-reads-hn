*** TLDR
OpenAI added two models below GPT-6 Astra. Sol costs $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens, half of GPT-5.6 Sol's $4 and $20. Luna costs $0.10 and $0.50, half of GPT-5.6 Luna's $0.20 and $1.20. Both use Astra's training methods plus work on caching and inference infrastructure. The claimed numbers: Sol hits 33.2% on AutomationBench at xhigh effort, above Claude Opus 5 at 9% of Opus 5's cost per task; 68.8% on DeepSWE v1.1 at max effort, 1.1 points behind Claude Fable 5 at roughly 80% lower cost; matches Opus 5 on OSWorld 2.0 at 80% lower cost per task; and makes about half as many factual mistakes as its predecessor. Both ship in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu, and in the API as gpt-6-sol and gpt-6-luna.

*** Take
Read the benchmark claims again and notice what they are measuring. Not one of them says Sol beats Anthropic's best model outright. Every one is a cost-per-task ratio: same score, a fifth of the price, or a tenth. That is the product. It landed a day after Opus 5.5 cut its own prices 40% and its cache reads from $0.50 to $0.20, which means two labs repriced against each other inside 24 hours. If you are choosing a model this week, the score tables matter less than which vendor's usage limits you can live with, which is exactly where jeffnash's comment ends up. And m_fayer's is the one that will age well: the thing people miss about a retired model is never its benchmark row.

*** TLDR
Trail of Bits argues SAML's security record comes from its architecture, not from sloppy implementations. Five structural problems: it is built on XML, so it inherits XXE, entity expansion and XPath injection; canonicalization requires the identity provider and service provider to produce byte-identical XML or the signature will not verify; signatures are enveloped, wrapped inside the document they sign, unlike JWT's detached signatures, which is what makes XML signature wrapping possible at all; the spec bundles four protocols and a pile of features almost nobody uses while roughly 99% of deployments share the same small subset; and it was designed for pre-HTTPS VPN-era networks and has not evolved, where OIDC added PKCE, DPoP and device grants through incremental RFCs. The post traces signature wrapping and parser differential bugs from 2012 to 2025, including recent breaks in Go's XML library and in GitHub Enterprise. Its recommendation is to stop building new SAML integrations and to plan deprecation of existing ones.

*** Take
The enveloped signature is the root cause and the rest follows from it. Once the signature lives inside the document it is signing, every disagreement between two XML parsers about what the document contains becomes an authentication bypass, and thirteen years of XSW CVEs are the evidence. What the post leaves out, cameronh90 supplies in the thread: OIDC has no IdP-initiated flow, and enterprise buyers ask for that one by name on the RFP. SAML will outlive this blog post, because the people who keep choosing it are not the people reading it.

***** Title
SAML: ein Fraktal schlechten Designs
***** TLDR
Trail of Bits argumentiert, dass SAMLs Sicherheitsbilanz aus der Architektur stammt, nicht aus schlampigen Implementierungen. Fünf strukturelle Probleme: Es baut auf XML auf und erbt damit XXE, Entity-Expansion und XPath-Injection. Die Kanonisierung verlangt, dass Identity Provider und Service Provider byteidentisches XML erzeugen, sonst schlägt die Signaturprüfung fehl. Signaturen sind umhüllend, sie liegen im signierten Dokument selbst, anders als JWTs abgetrennte Signaturen, und genau das macht XML Signature Wrapping überhaupt möglich. Die Spezifikation bündelt vier Protokolle und viele kaum genutzte Funktionen, während rund 99% der Installationen dieselbe kleine Teilmenge verwenden. Und sie wurde für VPN-Netze vor HTTPS entworfen und hat sich nicht weiterentwickelt, während OIDC per inkrementeller RFCs PKCE, DPoP und Device Grants nachzog. Der Beitrag zeichnet Signature-Wrapping- und Parser-Differential-Fehler von 2012 bis 2025 nach, darunter jüngste Brüche in Gos XML-Bibliothek und in GitHub Enterprise. Empfehlung: keine neuen SAML-Integrationen bauen und bestehende abkündigen.
***** Take
Die umhüllende Signatur ist die Ursache, alles andere folgt daraus. Sobald die Signatur im Dokument liegt, das sie signiert, wird jede Uneinigkeit zweier XML-Parser über den Inhalt zu einem Authentifizierungs-Bypass, und dreizehn Jahre XSW-CVEs sind der Beleg. Was der Beitrag auslässt, liefert cameronh90 im Thread: OIDC kennt keinen IdP-initiierten Flow, und Enterprise-Käufer fragen genau den namentlich in der Ausschreibung ab. SAML wird diesen Blogpost überleben, weil die Leute, die es weiter auswählen, nicht die sind, die ihn lesen.
