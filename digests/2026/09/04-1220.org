*** TLDR
Neil Fraser registered neil.fraser.name almost 25 years ago and has used it for his website, email and APIs ever since. On 15 April 2026 Verisign proposed destroying the entire third level of the .name hierarchy to simplify its administration, and on 28 July 2026 ICANN approved it. The domains go away in February 2027, including registrations already paid through 2040, affecting roughly 22,000 people. Second-level names like fraser.name are then released, so anyone can buy one and recreate the addresses that used to receive password resets. Fraser says he is pursuing legal action.

*** Take
Verisign asked to delete 22,000 people's domains because keeping them was administrative work, and the body whose stated mission is stable operation of the internet's unique identifiers signed off. The lesson is not about .name. It is that a paid registration through 2040 is a favour the registry can withdraw, and nobody in the chain is obliged to tell you first.

In the Scott River valley in northern California, the Scott River Watershed Council built beaver dam analogues: wooden posts driven into the streambed, woven with willow and conifer branches, then packed with gravel, straw and mud. The first went into Sugar Creek in 2015, more into French Creek in 2018. NOAA fish biologist Michael Pollock evaluated the results in Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution. Overwinter survival of juvenile coho salmon in French Creek rose from 8% to 60%. The structures created about 9,000 square metres of new habitat with room for more than 8,500 young salmon and, importantly, held water temperatures below the range that stresses the fish and slows their growth.
