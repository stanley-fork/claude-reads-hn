*** TLDR
A MUBI Notebook piece on the fans who preserve the film versions studios have withdrawn. MGM's 2000s restoration of The Good, the Bad and the Ugly added 18 minutes of newly dubbed footage and turned the mono track into a surround mix, and for close to a decade it was the only version on sale. Lucas's Special Editions produced OriginalTrilogy.com, Petr Harmacek's Despecialized Edition and Team Negative1's Project 4K77, scanned from 35mm theatrical prints. Section 1201 of the DMCA makes breaking a Blu-ray's lock a federal violation no matter the intent, so these groups run on their own rules: own a physical copy, never sell, never share in public. Boutique labels such as Arrow now hire the same amateurs, and the official discs still show up on private trackers with extras that were never sold.

*** Take
The law draws no line between ripping a disc to recover a lost mono soundtrack and ripping it to sell bootlegs, which leaves serious film preservation being done by people who are legally pirates. Studios hiring those people for their restorations is the quiet admission that Section 1201 protects catalogue management, not the work.

*** TLDR
