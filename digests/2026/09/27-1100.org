*** TLDR
The Authors Guild published its summary-judgment filing in Alter v. OpenAI and Microsoft, the class action in Manhattan federal court whose named plaintiffs include George R.R. Martin, John Grisham and Jonathan Franzen. The filing quotes internal material to argue the executives knew the training-data sourcing was illegal: policy director Jack Clark writing in May 2020 that artists would object and the company would likely ignore them and ship anyway, and researcher Tarun Gogineni describing displaced authors as acceptable economic disruption while working toward models that could finish Martin's series. It says Microsoft knew by April 2019 that OpenAI was using LibGen, described internally as a sketchy Russian website, and that this reached Bill Gates and Kevin Scott. The detail the HN thread seized on: in summer 2022 OpenAI ran "Project Clear" to delete LibGen files, motivated in part by the optics of coverage on sites like Hacker News. Oral argument on summary judgment is expected in early 2027.

*** Take
The interesting thing is not that OpenAI used LibGen, which has been public for years, but the timing of the cleanup. Deleting the corpus in 2022 while keeping the models trained on it is the behaviour of a company that thought the files were the liability. The Authors Guild is a lobbying organisation writing an advocacy post about its own brief, so read the framing accordingly, but the quotes are from discovery and OpenAI gets to contest them in front of a judge, not a comment section.

*** TLDR
The HN link is a Reddit thread, so the reporting comes from Agencia Brasil and Brazilian outlets. Meta disabled the advertising account attached to Lula's Facebook page in late September, roughly ten days before Brazil's first-round vote on 5 October. The account was restored the same day after the campaign appealed, and Meta gave the Workers' Party no explanation and did not respond to Agencia Brasil before publication. Lula's lawyers called the restriction unilateral and without cause, demanded human review by Meta's legal and compliance staff, and argued it put their candidate at a disadvantage against others. The party's communications secretary, Eden Valadares, said Brazil's elections cannot be left to the political preferences of big tech companies, and the party threatened to escalate to the Superior Electoral Court.

*** Take
The outage was short and the ads came back, which is exactly why this matters: a platform can suspend a sitting president's campaign advertising with no stated reason, restore it, and owe nobody an account of what happened. Brazil's Superior Electoral Court has spent years forcing platforms into disclosure rules, and this is the gap those rules still leave. If Meta has an automated enforcement system that can take a head of state's ad account offline ten days before a vote, publishing the trigger is the minimum, and the absence of an answer is what turns a moderation error into an election story.

*** TLDR
The author noticed Wikipedia and Google give Yemen's area as about 555,000 square kilometres, while the CIA World Factbook, the World Bank and the UN say roughly 528,000, and tracing the borders in Google Maps produced only about 456,000. Digging into where the numbers came from, they found two separate errors. The 195,000 square kilometres attributed to North Yemen traces to a 1904 description of an Ottoman province, not the independent country, which was closer to 136,000. And a 1976 World Bank report on South Yemen's economy double-counted the Thamud region, inflating South Yemen from 287,000 to 338,100. The author's corrected estimate is around 424,000 square kilometres, meaning the widely cited figure overstates Yemen by roughly a quarter.

*** Take

The practical lesson for anyone building on public datasets is that agreement between authorities is weak evidence, and the only real check is going back to where the number was first computed.

*** TLDR
GitHub's Primer team describes moving its design system off styled-components and onto CSS Modules. With CSS-in-JS, styles were initialised on the client and the cost of collecting them grew with the number of components on a page, which hurt both first load and server-side rendering. CSS Modules compile to static stylesheets shipped with the HTML, so the runtime work disappears and the CSS payload grows instead. They rolled it out behind feature flags with visual regression tests to prove the old and new styles matched, finishing the component migration in December 2024 and reporting a 55% cut in server-side rendering time and a 25% cut in component initialisation. Removing the sx prop across the product ran from April 2025 to May 2026, with per-page SSR gains between 1% and 22%, helped by a transitional @primer/styled-react wrapper, a VS Code plugin, a codemod, and Copilot coding agents from April 2026.

*** Take
The title is the honest version of a trade the industry spent a decade getting wrong: CSS-in-JS moved styling work from build time to every page load, and undoing it means shipping more bytes to do less work. The 55% SSR number is real and worth having. What the post does not address is the top comment's measurement, 41 requests and around 2 MB of CSS on a GitHub page, because a faster render of a large stylesheet is still a large stylesheet. The other thing worth noting is the timeline: eighteen months to delete one prop, which is the real cost of a design system decision made in 2018.

GitHub 的 Primer 团队讲述了把设计系统从 styled-components 迁移到 CSS Modules 的过程。使用 CSS-in-JS 时，样式在客户端初始化，而收集样式的开销随页面组件数量增长，这同时拖慢首次加载和服务端渲染。CSS Modules 会编译成随 HTML 一起发送的静态样式表，运行时开销消失，代价是 CSS 体积变大。他们在功能开关后面逐步推进，用视觉回归测试证明新旧样式一致，2024 年 12 月完成组件迁移，并报告服务端渲染时间下降 55%、组件初始化时间下降 25%。在整个产品中移除 sx prop 的工作从 2025 年 4 月持续到 2026 年 5 月，各页面的 SSR 收益在 1% 到 22% 之间，过程中用到了过渡包 @primer/styled-react、一个 VS Code 插件、一个代码改写工具，以及 2026 年 4 月起投入的 Copilot 编码 agent。
***** Take
这个标题诚实地概括了业界花了十年才做对的一笔交易：CSS-in-JS 把样式工作从构建期搬到了每一次页面加载，而把它撤回来就意味着传输更多字节去做更少的工作。55% 的 SSR 改善是真实的、值得拿下的收益。文章没有回应的是热评里的实测：一个 GitHub 页面上 41 个请求、约 2 MB 的 CSS——把一张大样式表渲染得更快，它仍然是一张大样式表。另一点值得注意的是时间线：删掉一个 prop 花了十八个月，这就是 2018 年一个设计系统决策的真实代价。

***** Title
Mejorar el rendimiento del sitio enviando más CSS
***** TLDR
El equipo Primer de GitHub cuenta cómo sacó su sistema de diseño de styled-components y lo pasó a CSS Modules. Con CSS-in-JS los estilos se inicializaban en el cliente y el coste de recolectarlos crecía con el número de componentes de la página, lo que perjudicaba tanto la primera carga como el renderizado en servidor. CSS Modules compila a hojas de estilo estáticas que viajan con el HTML, así que el trabajo en tiempo de ejecución desaparece y a cambio crece el peso del CSS. Desplegaron el cambio detrás de feature flags con pruebas de regresión visual para demostrar que los estilos viejos y nuevos coincidían, terminaron la migración de componentes en diciembre de 2024 y reportan una reducción del 55% en el tiempo de renderizado en servidor y del 25% en la inicialización de componentes. Eliminar la prop sx en todo el producto llevó de abril de 2025 a mayo de 2026, con mejoras de SSR por página de entre 1% y 22%, con ayuda del envoltorio transitorio @primer/styled-react, un plugin de VS Code, un codemod y agentes de codificación de Copilot desde abril de 2026.
***** Take
El título es la versión honesta de un intercambio que la industria tardó una década en entender: CSS-in-JS movió el trabajo de estilos del momento de compilación a cada carga de página, y deshacerlo significa enviar más bytes para hacer menos trabajo. El 55% de mejora en SSR es real y vale tenerlo. Lo que el artículo no aborda es la medición del comentario más votado, 41 peticiones y unos 2 MB de CSS en una página de GitHub, porque renderizar rápido una hoja de estilos enorme sigue dejándote una hoja de estilos enorme. Lo otro llamativo es el plazo: dieciocho meses para borrar una prop, que es el coste real de una decisión de sistema de diseño tomada en 2018.
***** Comments
- No sé cómo perciben el rendimiento. Veo 41 peticiones de red. Son 2,1 MB de CSS por el cable, bloqueando el renderizado y perjudicando el pintado y la carga. Hay 400 KB de Tailwind, 87 KB de CSS general y otros 200 KB de CSS general. Seguro que podrían tener un único archivo CSS por debajo de 80 KB.
- Uso GitHub a diario y no he notado una mejora de rendimiento. Diría que las páginas van cada vez más lentas. Navegar issues con muchos comentarios o PRs grandes es una experiencia terrible porque no se carga todo.
- Todavía hay margen de mejora. La build de producción usa nombres de clase largos de desarrollo. Algo como DirectoryContent-module__Box_3__gl6dE podría compilarse a un hash más corto.

***** Title
Seitenperformance verbessern, indem man mehr CSS ausliefert
***** TLDR
Das Primer-Team von GitHub beschreibt, wie es sein Designsystem von styled-components auf CSS Modules umgestellt hat. Bei CSS-in-JS wurden Styles im Client initialisiert, und die Kosten für das Sammeln der Styles wuchsen mit der Zahl der Komponenten auf einer Seite, was sowohl den ersten Seitenaufbau als auch das Server-Side-Rendering belastete. CSS Modules kompilieren zu statischen Stylesheets, die mit dem HTML ausgeliefert werden, wodurch die Laufzeitarbeit verschwindet und stattdessen die CSS-Menge wächst. Sie führten die Umstellung hinter Feature Flags mit visuellen Regressionstests ein, um die Gleichheit von alten und neuen Styles zu belegen, schlossen die Komponentenmigration im Dezember 2024 ab und berichten von 55% weniger Server-Side-Rendering-Zeit und 25% weniger Komponenten-Initialisierung. Das Entfernen der sx-Prop im Produkt lief von April 2025 bis Mai 2026, mit SSR-Verbesserungen von 1% bis 22% pro Seite, unterstützt vom Übergangspaket @primer/styled-react, einem VS-Code-Plugin, einem Codemod und ab April 2026 von Copilot-Coding-Agenten.
***** Take
Der Titel ist die ehrliche Fassung eines Tauschs, den die Branche ein Jahrzehnt lang falsch bewertet hat: CSS-in-JS verlagerte Styling-Arbeit von der Buildzeit in jeden Seitenaufruf, und die Rücknahme bedeutet, mehr Bytes zu schicken, um weniger Arbeit zu tun. Die 55% bei SSR sind echt und es wert. Was der Beitrag nicht behandelt, ist die Messung im Top-Kommentar: 41 Requests und rund 2 MB CSS auf einer GitHub-Seite. Ein großes Stylesheet schneller zu rendern lässt es ein großes Stylesheet bleiben. Bemerkenswert ist außerdem der Zeitrahmen: achtzehn Monate, um eine einzige Prop zu löschen. Das sind die tatsächlichen Kosten einer Designsystem-Entscheidung aus dem Jahr 2018.

Da sind 400 KB Tailwind, 87 KB allgemeines CSS und weitere 200 KB allgemeines CSS. Sicher wäre eine einzige CSS-Datei unter 80 KB möglich gewesen.
