*** TLDR
Economist Matt Clancy argues that the Industrial Revolution is an overstated precedent for AI-driven explosive growth. Before the IR, 10x faster growth (0.66%/yr vs 0.07%/yr long-run average) occurred in 46% of years - it was just a good harvest becoming more common. Today, 10x faster growth (19%/yr) has occurred in only 0.76% of country-years since 1950, mostly from oil windfalls or post-war recovery. An IR-style acceleration today would take growth to ~2.8%/yr, not 20%.

*** Take
Someone finally did the math on the 'but the Industrial Revolution!' argument and it turns out comparing 0.07% baseline to 1% isn't the same as going from 2% to 20%. Who knew that context matters?

*** TLDR
A ClickHouse engineer instrumented a Gaggia espresso machine running GaggiMate (open-source ESP32 controller) with OpenTelemetry. Each shot is a trace with child spans for brew phases, metrics track temperature/pressure/flow, and derived metrics like channeling detection (coefficient of variation of puck resistance) are computed on-device. The telemetry ships to ClickStack where you can trace individual bad shots back to their pressure curves weeks later.

*** Take
Finally, observability for the truly mission-critical systems. Your Kubernetes cluster can wait, but a sour espresso demands immediate root cause analysis.
