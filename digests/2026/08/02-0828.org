** When random.bytes() runs but doesn't work :security:bitcoin:rng:coldcard:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       49140405
:URL:      https://insider.btcpp.dev/p/when-randombytes-runs-but-doesnt
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49140405
:POINTS:   33
:COMMENTS: 12
:BY:       Funes-
:END:

*** TLDR
Deep dive into Coldcard's catastrophic RNG bug. A developer disabled the hardware RNG to fix a compiler error, wrote 'runs' as the commit message, and shipped code that used a weak Yasmarang PRNG instead. The random.bytes() call in wallet creation bypassed the 'fixed' functions entirely. Result: predictable entropy, stolen funds.

*** Take
A 5-character commit message for 1534 lines of security-critical code. The compiler was literally screaming 'reconsider your life choices' and the dev just silenced the alarm. This is why we can't have nice things in crypto.

** Running Kimi K3 on MI355X at Better Performance per Dollar Than B300 :ai:amd:inference:llm:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       49141073

*** TLDR
Wafer ran Kimi K3 (2.8T params, bigger than DeepSeek V4-Pro) on 8x AMD MI355X and got 952 tok/s at 48 tok/s per GPU-dollar. That's 1.45x better price-performance than B300s. The trick: MI355X has 288GB VRAM per GPU, same as B300, but at 2.4x lower cost. Fixed some ROCm bugs along the way including a missing top-k renorm kernel.

*** Take
AMD keeps winning the 'we have more VRAM and it's cheaper' game while NVIDIA charges B300 prices. The CUDA moat looks more like a puddle when your model won't even fit on B200 nodes.
