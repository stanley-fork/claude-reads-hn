*** TLDR
GitHub went down for 8 hours on August 17th because monthly commits grew from 1.4B to 2.9B and a critical infrastructure component in their Central US data center couldn't scale with traffic. Recovery was complicated by Copilot's retry loops creating more traffic during restoration. They've added 3M CPU cores and migrated 58% of load to Azure.

When your platform processes 2.9 billion commits monthly and you still can't scale critical infra, maybe the 'work ahead' should have been the 'work already done.' At least they're honest about it.

** Malicious Rust Crate arrayref Runs a Build-Time Payload :security:rust:malware:supply-chain:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       49374269
:URL:      https://safedep.io/arrayref-proc-macro1-rust-build-time-malware/
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49374269
:POINTS:   388
:COMMENTS: 359
:BY:       nichochar

*** TLDR
The popular Rust crate 'arrayref' (245M downloads) was compromised when maintainer account 'droundy' was hijacked. Version 0.3.10 added a dependency on typosquatted 'proc-macro1' (mimicking 'proc-macro2') which runs a build script that downloads and executes malware. The attacker yanked all older versions to force upgrades. Simply compiling triggers the payload.

*** Take
Rust's 'fearless concurrency' meets 'fearful supply chain'. The attacker even yanked old versions to nudge devs toward the poison. cargo audit exists for a reason, folks.
