*** TLDR
Google released an open-source tool called HEIR that uses fully homomorphic encryption to run ML inference on encrypted data. The compiler framework converts TensorFlow/JAX models to work on encrypted inputs, so sensitive data never needs to be decrypted during processing. They've achieved 100x-1000x speedups over previous FHE approaches, making it actually usable for real applications like password monitoring.

*** Take
We've been hearing 'FHE is 5 years away' for about 15 years now. But Google shipping actual tools with 1000x speedups might mean we're finally getting close. The password breach checker use case is clever since it solves the 'trust us bro' problem of sending your passwords to a third party.

*** TLDR
Dmitry.gr, a seasoned embedded systems developer, tears into RISC-V's design decisions. His main gripes: the compressed instruction extension (RVC) breaks alignment guarantees making disassembly a nightmare, there's no hardware division by default, the privilege model is overcomplicated, and the extension system is fragmenting the ecosystem into incompatible variants. He argues ARM did most of this better decades ago.

*** Take
The RISC-V hype train has been running on 'open source means good' vibes for years. Nice to see someone who actually writes assembly point out that 'minimal and elegant' sometimes means 'we left out useful stuff and made your life harder.' The RVC alignment complaint is particularly brutal.
