** os8088 – A Mac-style GUI OS for the IBM PC XT :retrocomputing:os:assembly:hardware:

*** TLDR
A hobby OS that boots from floppy into a graphical desktop with overlapping windows, pull-down menus, and preemptive multitasking - all on an 8086/8088 CPU. The 78KB kernel is written entirely in real-mode assembly (112K lines of NASM), supports VGA/Hercules/CGA displays, and runs multiple programs simultaneously with 18.2Hz context switching. Includes games like Minesweeper, a task manager with CPU graphs, and actually runs on original 1981 IBM hardware.

*** Take
Someone built what Apple couldn't in 1984 - preemptive multitasking on period hardware - and did it as a hobby project 40 years late. The 'could have been' is doing a lot of heavy lifting here, but respect for shipping 112K lines of assembly instead of just posting about it on HN.

*** TLDR
Amit Patel's latest interactive tutorial explains how to speed up A⁎ pathfinding by precomputing distances to 'landmark' points. The trick: if you know the distance from everywhere to a few strategic landmarks, you can use the triangle inequality to get a tighter lower bound for the actual path cost. The closer your heuristic is to the true distance, the fewer nodes A⁎ has to explore. Works especially well in games with constrained maps like corridors and rooms.

** Dithered QR Codes :qr:graphics:dithering:creative-coding:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       49226742
:URL:      https://www.andrewt.net/dithered-qr-codes/wtf/
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49226742
:POINTS:   115
:COMMENTS: 13
:BY:       atum47
:END:

*** TLDR
A technique for embedding photos inside QR codes using Floyd-Steinberg error diffusion dithering. The key insight: QR data modules create salt-and-pepper noise in the image, but if you treat those modules as fixed constraints and diffuse their 'error' to surrounding pixels BEFORE normal dithering, the noise becomes nearly invisible. The result is a one-bit image with QR alignment patterns that actually scans. Includes a generator tool and notes on the tradeoff between aesthetics and scannability.

*** Take
Finally, a way to make QR codes that don't look like they were designed by a committee of engineers who actively hate beauty. The error-diffusion-before-dithering trick is genuinely clever. Now someone just needs to make these work reliably on crumpled paper.
