*** TLDR
Git 2.54/2.55 introduced an experimental 'git history' command with fixup, reword, and split subcommands. It lets you amend old commits and automatically rebases ALL branches that descend from that commit - not just the current one. It's atomic (refuses to proceed if conflicts arise) and delivers many jj benefits without switching your whole workflow.

*** Take
Every 3 months someone tries jj, gives up, and goes back to git. Finally, git is stealing jj's homework and putting it in core. The irony of 'experimental' being more stable than your production rebases.
