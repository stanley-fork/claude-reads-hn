** Measuring Input Latency on Linux: X11 vs. Wayland, VRR, and DXVK :linux:gaming:wayland:latency:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       48909424
:URL:      https://marco-nett.de/blog/measuring-input-latency-on-linux-x11-vs-wayland-vrr-dxvk/
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48909424
:POINTS:   278
:COMMENTS: 152
:BY:       hoechst
:END:

*** TLDR
A developer built a hardware device to measure end-to-end input latency on Linux gaming. Key findings: X11 beats native Wayland by only 0.14-0.22ms. VRR provides the biggest benefit (0.26-0.45ms faster). XWayland adds a catastrophic 3.13ms of latency. The dxvk-low-latency fork helps most in uncapped scenarios.

*** Take
Finally someone did the actual science instead of posting 'Wayland feels off' for the 47th time. Turns out native Wayland is basically X11 with better vibes. XWayland is the real villain here, adding more latency than every other optimization combined.

*** TLDR
PrismML released Bonsai 27B, based on Qwen3.6 27B. The ternary version (1.71 bits/weight) is 5.9GB and retains 95% of full-precision quality. The 1-bit version (1.125 bits/weight) is 3.9GB and actually fits on an iPhone 17 Pro. Both support 262K context, multimodal vision, tool calling, and speculative decoding. Apple is reportedly in acquisition talks.

*** Take
We've gone from 'you need a datacenter' to 'here's a 27B model on your phone' in about 18 months. The 1-bit model scores better than conventional 4-bit builds while using 2.5x less memory. Intelligence density is the new metric, and PrismML is speedrunning it.
