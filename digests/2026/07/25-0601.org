*** TLDR
Someone built the world's first open source ethernet switch ASIC and is taping it out on the Tiny Tapeout shuttle. It's a 3-port, 100Mbps, cut-through, unmanaged switch that interfaces with LAN8720A PHY chips over RMII. The chip occupies 711x325um and silicon arrives mid-November.

*** Take
The networking equivalent of home-brewing your own CPU. While everyone's busy making RISC-V chips, this absolute unit decided to build something nobody asked for but everybody needed. Less sexy than a CPU, infinitely more useful for your home lab.

*** TLDR
In 1985, MIT professor William Freeman patented a three-sided zipper that could turn floppy strips into rigid structures. The proposal was rejected and sat in his garage for 40 years. Now MIT CSAIL revived it with 3D printing, creating software that designs custom Y-zippers that transform between soft and rigid states. Applications include pop-up tents (1 min 20 sec vs 6 min setup), adjustable wrist casts, and robots that change leg length.

*** Take
The future was sitting in some guy's garage the whole time. Took 40 years for manufacturing to catch up to the idea. Now your tent can shape-shift like a Transformer, which is either revolutionary or extremely annoying depending on how much you trust 3D-printed plastics.
