*** TLDR
TheNumbers.com, the definitive film box office database for 30 years, was taken down in March by a combination of relentless AI bot scraping (90% of traffic was non-human) and suspected hackers probing for prediction market advantages. The site had to rebuild from scratch on new infrastructure because restoring the old 160,000-file legacy codebase would have been instantly compromised again. Only 10% of their traffic comes from actual humans now.

*** Take
The prediction market angle is genuinely terrifying. Someone realized they could make money on Polymarket by getting box office numbers a few hours early, and apparently decided that hacking a small film database was a reasonable business strategy. We've reached the point where hobbyist movie stats sites need enterprise-grade security because gamblers exist.

A tutorial series that teaches you how OpenGL, Vulkan, Metal, and DirectX work by building a software renderer from scratch in ~500 lines of C++. No graphics libraries allowed. You start with a single function to set pixel colors and end up rendering textured 3D models with lighting. The author's students typically complete it in 10-20 hours.

*** Take
This is how graphics programming should be taught. Instead of drowning in API calls and driver quirks, you learn what's actually happening when triangles become pixels. The 'no third-party libraries' constraint is chef's kiss. Modern devs could use more of this 'build it yourself to understand it' energy.
