** The art and engineering of Sega CD Silpheed :retro-gaming:reverse-engineering:sega:fmv:compression:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       48893639
:URL:      https://fabiensanglard.net/silpheed/index.html
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48893639
:POINTS:   163
:COMMENTS: 32
:BY:       ibobev
:END:

*** TLDR
Fabien Sanglard spent two weeks reverse-engineering Silpheed's FMV format. The game ran near-fullscreen cutscenes on a 12.5MHz m68k using just 16 colors and 150 KiB/s bandwidth. The secret: exploit tilemaps to compress video, with half the tiles being single-color references and two-color tiles generated using the Mega-CD ASIC's 'font bit' feature. Game Arts worked bottom-up from hardware constraints while FMV competitors worked top-down from movies, which is why Silpheed looked incredible and Night Trap looked like a VHS left in the sun.

*** Take
This is the kind of engineering porn where you learn that Silpheed artists designed gameplay sequences using only 12 colors because four were reserved for laser palette cycling. Meanwhile, most FMV games couldn't even use 256 colors without looking like pixel soup.
