*** TLDR
EPFL researchers built NEvo, a system that evolves AI-generated videos to maximally activate specific brain regions. It trains a 'digital twin' neural network to predict brain responses, then uses evolutionary algorithms to mutate video prompts (subject, lighting, motion, mood) across generations. The resulting videos outperform both natural videos and handcrafted stimuli. Key finding: moving up the lateral visual stream from V1 to aSTS, the synthesized stimuli drift from abstract patterns toward faces and social interactions, mapping how vision becomes increasingly social.

*** Take
They built a video generator that figures out what makes each part of your visual cortex maximally excited. The face-recognition area gets faces, the place area gets places, the social area gets people interacting. It's useful neuroscience research, but let's not pretend this won't eventually be used to create the most addictive TikTok content known to humanity.

** Life with Hazard Ratios :statistics:health:longevity:math:
:PROPERTIES:

*** TLDR
A hazard ratio of 0.9 doesn't mean 10% more life expectancy. The article explains why, using the Keyfitz entropy concept: for US males, the 'discount factor' is about 0.17, so HR=0.9 translates to roughly 1.3 extra years, not 7.5. The math gets complicated because hazard ratios at different ages have different impacts, and papers usually report a single constant number that's actually a weighted average. The good news: despite all these caveats, you can sorta-mostly convert paper HRs to life expectancy changes using a provided curve.

*** Take
Finally, someone explains why 'eating kale reduces mortality by 20%' doesn't mean you'll live 20% longer. The Keyfitz entropy discount is brutal: we're basically playing Russian roulette with compressed mortality distributions. Also notable: mice have a much higher Keyfitz entropy (~0.45 vs ~0.15), which might explain why every mouse longevity intervention fails in humans.
