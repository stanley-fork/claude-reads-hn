*** TLDR
Dex Horthy argues that 'lights-off' software factories where AI writes and reviews all code don't actually work. His team tried it in July 2025, got bitten by gnarly bugs the agents couldn't solve, and eventually rewrote from scratch. The core problem: models degrade codebase quality over time because there's no RL reward for good design, only for passing tests. Claude Code won by training inside its harness, but no amount of loopsmaxxing fixes the fundamental issue that models can't maintain software architecture.

*** Take
The article that should be required reading before anyone at your company proposes 'just let the AI ship everything.' Turns out the bottleneck was never typing speed.

** A solid-state 'atomic channel' for separating rare earth elements :chemistry:materials-science:rare-earths:sustainability:

:ID:       49025831

:COMMENTS: 15

*** TLDR
UChicago researchers discovered a cleaner method to separate rare earth elements using manganese oxide with precisely engineered atomic channels. Instead of toxic organic solvents and massive amounts of acid, this uses water and electricity. They exploit the size difference in water shells around different lanthanides, and by adding magnesium ions to 'pin' the channels at exact widths, achieved 97% pure neodymium in two cycles. Could enable rare earth processing to happen locally instead of shipping everything overseas.

*** Take
Finally, chemistry doing something genuinely useful for the planet instead of inventing a new forever chemical. The magnesium 'pinning' trick is elegant.
