** EEG shows brain can simultaneously encode two speech streams :neuroscience:research:cognition:attention:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       48943745
:URL:      https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3003876
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48943745
:POINTS:   56
:COMMENTS: 23
:BY:       giuliomagnifico
:END:

*** TLDR
Researchers used EEG to study how the brain handles attention switching between two speakers. They found that when you switch attention, your brain starts tracking the new speaker BEFORE fully disengaging from the old one - a brief period of genuine parallel processing. They also used LLMs to model how lexical context is rebuilt after a switch, finding the brain essentially does a hard reset on context rather than trying to blend the two streams.

*** Take
Finally, scientific validation that you really can half-listen to your partner while scrolling HN. The catch: you're not actually understanding either conversation, just briefly encoding both before your brain picks a winner.

*** TLDR
In 1984, Digital Research Inc released StarLink, a hardware/software combo that turned an IBM PC into a multi-user system serving up to 5 users via dumb terminals. It cost $1,695 and required Concurrent DOS. The product failed because it couldn't run Lotus 1-2-3, the killer app of the era. The team that built it later left to start Ventura Software (of Ventura Publisher fame).

*** Take
Before Elon's satellite internet, StarLink was DRI's attempt to make PCs share-economy before that was a thing. It died because it couldn't run spreadsheets - the eternal fate of any tech that doesn't play nice with Excel's ancestors.
