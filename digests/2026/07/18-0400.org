** The Zilog Z80 has turned 50 :hardware:history:retrocomputing:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       48951461

*** TLDR
The Z80 launched in July 1976, created by Federico Faggin and Masatoshi Shima who left Intel after frustrating delays on the 8080. It was binary compatible with the 8080 but added index registers, bank-switched registers, and simpler electrical interfacing (single 5V supply, single clock). Used in everything from ZX Spectrum to GameBoy (via Sharp's LR35902 derivative) to industrial controllers. Zilog discontinued it in June 2024 after nearly 50 years of production.

*** Take
The chip that powered more childhood memories than any therapist could process, and somehow outlasted three marriages of corporate ownership. It's almost poetic that a processor designed by engineers fleeing Intel's bureaucracy ended up being too useful to die.

** TP-Link Kasa cameras leaked home GPS via unauthenticated UDP for 6 years :security:iot:privacy:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       48952565
:URL:      https://github.com/BadChemical/IoT-Vulnerability-Research-Public/blob/main/TP-Link_Kasa_EC71/Kasa_EC71.md
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48952565
:POINTS:   58
:COMMENTS: 10
:BY:       BadChemical
:END:

*** TLDR
TP-Link Kasa EC71 cameras exposed precise GPS coordinates of owners' homes via unauthenticated UDP requests to port 9999 since at least 2020. A single packet with trivial XOR cipher returned home location, hardware IDs, and firmware info. The protocol flaw was known since 2016 but TP-Link only patched cameras in 2026. Also found: fleet-wide hardcoded RSA keys and unsalted MD5 password hashes enabling cross-platform account takeover. Secondhand devices could reveal previous owners' home addresses.

*** Take
Six years of broadcasting your home coordinates to anyone on your WiFi, protected by encryption a teenager could break in a weekend project. TP-Link knew about this in their smart plug line and just... didn't check if cameras had the same bug. The secondhand market attack path is especially spicy: buy a used camera, extract the previous owner's location and credentials.

** Topcoat: The full full-stack framework for Rust :rust:webdev:framework:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       48952067

:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48952067
:POINTS:   55

*** TLDR
Topcoat is a batteries-included Rust web framework from the tokio-rs team, aiming to be Django/Rails for Rust. Components can be async and query databases directly. Uses a view! macro that transpiles to both server-rendered HTML and client-side JavaScript without WASM. Features module-based routing (like Next.js), built-in Tailwind, asset bundling, htmx integration, and 'shards' that re-render on the server when arguments change. Early stage, expect breaking changes.

*** Take
Every six months someone announces 'Django for Rust' and it's usually three macros and a dream. This one's from the tokio-rs team though, which means it might actually ship. The dual server/client rendering without WASM is genuinely clever, even if the phrase 'full full-stack' makes my eye twitch.
