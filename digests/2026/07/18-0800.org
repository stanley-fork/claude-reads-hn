*** TLDR
Progressive JPEGs load low-frequency components first so you see a blurry preview while downloading. This hack exploits scan headers to concatenate multiple images into one file that switches between them as it loads. Chrome renders about 90 frames before giving up, enough to embed a full video in a single JPEG with no JavaScript.

*** Take
Finally, a use case for progressive JPEG that isn't 'my grandma on dial-up.' Someone weaponized a 90s image format into the world's worst video codec and I respect that energy.

*** TLDR
Lego instructions evolved from simple 1955 drawings to today's LEGO Builder app. Key milestones: 1967-2003 outsourced to Palle Munch in Denmark who hand-drew them, 1986 introduced 'Panter' digital tool (cut production from 3 weeks to 3 days), 2003 brought in-house with 3D Vision, and 2022 moved to LDD Pro. The team grew from 2-3 people in 1983 to 100+ today. The 'build together' feature lets families split tasks on the same set.

*** Take
100+ people working on making instructions clearer, and I still manage to put pieces upside down. The real revelation: there was a 1960 internal debate about whether instructions 'spoil' children's creativity. Turns out they went with 'actually finishing things is also good.'
