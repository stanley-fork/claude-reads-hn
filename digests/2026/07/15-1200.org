*** TLDR
SIDN Labs research analyzed 'small' RPKI publication servers (under 1,300 ROAs). Found they're run by RPKIaaS providers, cross-RIR consolidators, researchers, and hobbyists. 92.4% of ROAs validate correctly, but 53.98% use maxLength contrary to RFC 9319, with 19.6% potentially vulnerable to sub-prefix hijacks. The tiny corner of Internet trust infrastructure includes government domains and services people actually rely on.

*** Take
The Internet's routing security literally depends on a bunch of nerds running Krill because they think it's 'pretty cool.' Meanwhile, a spam operator is using RPKI servers to add friction to abuse takedowns. Peak Internet.

** DSLs Enable Reliable Use of LLMs :ai:programming:dsl:llm:
:PROPERTIES:

:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48918575

*** TLDR
Martin Fowler argues LLMs work best with Domain-Specific Languages because constrained syntax reduces hallucination space. DSLs ship with validators (parsers, schemas, compilers) that let agents self-correct. He demos Tickloom, a semantic model for distributed systems testing where prompts like 'reproduce DDIA quorum read' generate working failure scenarios. Two-phase approach: LLM as brainstorming partner during DSL design, then natural-language interface once vocabulary exists.

*** Take
Fowler basically said 'give LLMs training wheels and they stop crashing' but took 4,000 words to say it. Still, he's right. Your vague English prompt is the problem, not the model.
